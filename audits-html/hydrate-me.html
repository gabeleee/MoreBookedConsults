Booking

Your “What brings you in?” question, turned into a form that books

You already ask the right first question: more energy, managing weight, aging well, or refreshing your appearance. But each answer leads to a “Learn more” page, not a booking. “Book Now” instead leaves your site for Zenoti, asks for a spa, then shows 15 category tabs before a visitor can pick a service. Four changes shorten that path, starting in month 1.

1Start with their goalYour own question first, in plain words, then a short list of treatments for that goal.
2Stay on your siteNo handoff to Zenoti's pages. Bookings still land in Zenoti, so the front desk changes nothing.
3Spa and time lastNearest spa in one tap, then name, email and mobile, then a time. In A/B tests I ran at LaserAway, easy questions first led more people to finish.
4On every pageThe same form ends every treatment and spa page, with the treatment or spa already chosen.
Topical authority · starts month 1

Make Hydrate Me known for aesthetics, not just IV

Of your 20 blog posts, 8 are about IV therapy and 3 are about injectables. That's why Google sees Hydrate Me as an IV brand: it ranks what you write about. A topical map plans one clear guide per question people ask before booking Botox, fillers or skin treatments, each linked to the treatment and spa pages where they book.

This runs alongside the page build from month 1. Your team's only job is approving guides.

Balance the brandNew aesthetics guides tell Google Hydrate Me is a med spa, not only an IV bar.
Every guide feeds a bookingLinks run from each guide to the treatment and spa pages, and every guide ends with the booking form.
New pages rank fasterOnce a topic is fully covered, Google trusts new pages on it sooner, including the new treatment pages.
