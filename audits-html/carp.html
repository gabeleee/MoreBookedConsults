Consult booking

Book either office in one short form

Today “Schedule Today!” opens a scheduler that asks for a visit type and a reason from two dropdowns, then shows times for the Uniontown office only, with the earliest six days out. Beachwood patients have to call. Pricing questions go to a separate quote form, and the VIP scheduling page has no form at all. Four changes make a consult easier to book, starting in month 1.

1Both offices onlineBeachwood patients can start a consult on the web, so Cleveland patients never have to call to begin.
2Procedure first, office secondPatients pick what they want in plain words, not a visit-type code, then the office closest to them.
3Easy questions first, time lastTwo quick taps, then name, email and mobile, then a time. In A/B tests I ran at LaserAway, easy questions first led more people to finish.
4On every procedure pageThe same form ends every procedure and city page, with the procedure and office already chosen.
Topical authority · starts month 1

Make Carp the name Northeast Ohio finds when researching surgery

Before anyone books a tummy tuck or a breast augmentation consult, they search questions: what recovery is like, what it costs in Cleveland, how to choose a surgeon. Carp has 53 blog posts written one at a time, with overlaps (three on gynecomastia, two each on Cellfina and mommy makeovers), dated posts like “Lose Weight in 2018”, and nothing on recovery or cost. A topical map plans one clear guide per question, each linked to the procedure and city pages where people book, including the new Cleveland and Beachwood pages.

This runs alongside the page build from month 1. Your team's only job is approving guides.

One guide per questionOverlapping posts merge into one strong guide, with redirects, so they stop competing with each other.
