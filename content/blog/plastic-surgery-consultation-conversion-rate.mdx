- **It's a chain of rates,** not one: visitor, inquiry, consult, surgery.
- **Median blended:** 0.5 to 1 percent of visitors become surgery.

Short answer: a median plastic surgery practice converts **30 to 50 percent of consultations into surgery,** and 60 percent or more is strong.

Upstream, a healthy practice turns **2 to 4 percent of website visitors into an inquiry** and **40 to 60 percent of inquiries into a booked consultation.**

Multiply those and the average site turns a low single-digit fraction of visitors into cases, which is exactly why small improvements at the middle of the funnel are worth so much.

## <span className="he">📊</span> The four-stage funnel

A plastic surgery practice doesn't have one conversion rate; it has a chain of them. Each stage is a separate benchmark and a separate fix.

| Stage | Poor | Median | Good |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visitor → inquiry | under 1.5% | 2 to 4% | 6%+ |
| Inquiry → booked consult | under 30% | 40 to 60% | 70%+ |
| Consult → surgery | under 25% | 30 to 50% | 60%+ |
| Visitor → surgery (blended) | under 0.3% | 0.5 to 1% | 1.5%+ |

Judge each stage on its own row. A practice with great surgeons and a 30% inquiry-to-consult rate doesn't have a talent problem. It has a follow-up problem.

## <span className="he">🎯</span> Why website conversion is the cheapest stage to fix

The visitor-to-inquiry stage is the only one you can improve **without spending a dollar more on ads and without adding staff.** It's just the site: [inquiry forms](/blog/plastic-surgery-website-design/), procedure pages, [before/after galleries](/blog/plastic-surgery-before-after-galleries/), and [financing clarity](/blog/plastic-surgery-financing-marketing/). That's why our [plastic surgery CRO](/plastic-surgery-cro/) work starts here: the leverage is highest and the cost is lowest.

## <span className="he">🔧</span> What moves each stage

Each stage has its own set of levers, and confusing them is how practices spend on the wrong fix.

<Steps>

<Step title="Visitor to inquiry">

Shorter forms, clearer procedure pricing signals, and ungated [before/after galleries](/blog/plastic-surgery-before-after-galleries/). This is pure website work, no ad spend required.

</Step>

<Step title="Inquiry to consult">

This stage is won or lost on speed and ownership: [speed-to-lead and follow-up sequences](/blog/plastic-surgery-lead-management/), and a [patient coordinator](/blog/patient-coordinator-consult-conversion/) who owns the booking. An inquiry that waits a day for a reply is often already gone.

</Step>

<Step title="Consult to surgery">

Here the levers are [financing presented well](/blog/plastic-surgery-financing-marketing/) and [virtual consult options](/blog/virtual-plastic-surgery-consultations/) for out-of-market patients, plus the in-consult experience itself.

</Step>

</Steps>

<Callout type="key">

The middle stage, inquiry to consult, is where most surgical practices bleed the most, and it's almost always a follow-up speed problem, not a marketing one.

Fixing it usually costs nothing but a faster process.

</Callout>

## <span className="he">🧮</span> Why the middle of the funnel is worth so much

The multiplication is what makes mid-funnel gains so valuable, so it's worth seeing in numbers.

Take a practice with 2,000 monthly visitors, a 2 percent inquiry rate, a 40 percent inquiry-to-consult rate, and a 40 percent consult-to-surgery rate.

<Callout type="stat">

Lift just the inquiry-to-consult stage from 40 to 60 percent, purely a follow-up-speed fix that costs nothing, and the same 40 inquiries produce 24 consults and roughly 10 surgeries. At surgical case values, those four extra surgeries a month can be worth more than the entire marketing budget.

</Callout>

You didn't buy a single extra visitor or inquiry. You stopped leaking the ones you already had at the stage where a faster process is the only cost.

That's the whole argument for measuring each stage separately: you can't fix a leak you haven't located, and the most expensive leak is rarely where owners assume.

## <span className="he">📏</span> How to measure without fooling yourself

Surgical decision cycles run 3 to 12 months, so last-click attribution lies.

A consult that closes into surgery in March may trace back to a website visit in December, and a naive report will credit whatever the patient clicked last.

Track cohorts by inquiry month, not by close month, and give each cohort a full window to mature before you judge it.

<Callout type="note">

Call tracking matters more here than for a med spa, because a large share of high-intent surgical inquiries come by phone.

An untracked call is an invisible conversion, and it makes your best channel look like your worst.

</Callout>
