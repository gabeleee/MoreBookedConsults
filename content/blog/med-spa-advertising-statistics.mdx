## <span className="he">📊</span> The headline numbers

We collected every U.S. med spa ad we could find in Meta's public Ad Library on October 4, 2026, and read the copy, format, run dates and destination of each one.

After cleaning, the sample held 570 unique ads from 184 advertisers: 16 national chains and 168 independent practices.

The 570 ads are unique pieces of copy.

Together they ran as 1,666 individual ads, because advertisers often run the same copy with different images, placements or audiences.

National chains supplied 362 of the 570 ads and independents supplied 208.

Most independents (84.5%) ran a single piece of ad copy, while the median chain ran 14.

## <span className="he">💵</span> Prices and offers

24.7% of med spa ads showed a dollar price in the copy, such as "$10/unit" or "$299 for half a syringe."

Independent med spas showed a price in 46.2% of their ads, almost four times the national chain rate of 12.4%.

Across advertisers, 48.4% showed a price in at least one ad.

Chains leaned on other kinds of offers.

12.4% of chain ads advertised a percent-off discount, against 2.9% of independent ads.

The word "free" (a free consult, a free add-on) appeared in 15.1% of all ads.

Deadline wording such as "limited time" or "this week only" appeared in 10.0% of ads, 14.4% for independents and 7.5% for chains.

### Advertised Botox price per unit

27 advertisers put a per-unit Botox price in at least one ad.

Their median advertised price was $10 per unit (October 2026, one figure per advertiser).

The middle half advertised between $8.50 and $11 per unit, and the full range ran from $7.49 to $13.

44% of those advertisers advertised a price under $10.

25 of the 27 were independent practices, so this is mostly the price of an independent's Botox special.

It's the promotional price in the ad, often with a minimum purchase, an asterisk or a new-patient condition, not the average price patients pay.

### Offers by treatment

Neurotoxin ads were the most price-led.

50.7% of the 67 Botox and neurotoxin ads showed a dollar price, and 38.8% led with a per-unit or per-session price.

Laser hair removal ads rarely showed a price (6.3%), but 51.6% still carried an offer.

Those offers were usually a free consult or bonus (20.0%), a percent or dollar discount (17.9%) or a package (12.6%), the highest package share of any treatment.

Body contouring ads were the least offer-driven, with 63.8% making no offer at all.

Filler ads split between a price (18.2% led with one), a free consult or bonus (14.5%) and a discount (14.5%).

## <span className="he">🎥</span> Image, video or dynamic creative

Dynamic creative, where Meta mixes several images, videos and headlines into one ad, was the most common format at 40.5% of ads.

Video made up 29.3%, a single image 17.2% and a carousel 8.2%.

Chains and independents used very different mixes.

The share of ads that included any video was almost the same for both groups: 32.0% for chains and 34.6% for independents.

The bigger difference is how much each group varies an ad.

Chain ads were more than three times as likely as independent ads to run in several versions at once.

## <span className="he">⏳</span> How long med spa ads run

81.6% of the ads were still running on October 4, 2026.

How long they had been running depended heavily on who ran them.

Live ads from national chains had been running a median of 25 days.

Live ads from independent med spas had been running a median of 210 days.

78.4% of live independent ads had been running for 90 days or more, against 31.4% of live chain ads.

27.6% of live independent ads had been running for a year or more, against 2.5% of live chain ads.

Among chain ads that had already stopped, the median ad ran 31 days (82 ads).

A long run doesn't mean an ad is working.

It means it was left on, and the Ad Library doesn't show spend or results, so these figures describe how advertisers manage ads rather than which ads perform.

The pattern fits the rest of the data: chains rotate many versions and retire them within about a month, while most independents run one ad and leave it on.

## <span className="he">🔗</span> Where the click goes

National chains sent 79.3% of their ads to their own website or landing page.

Independent med spas did that 44.2% of the time.

More than half of independent ads (53.4%) sent people to a Facebook page, a chat, an Instagram profile or no link at all.

Only 1.8% of all ads linked straight to an online booking page.

"Book now" was the most common button at 44.6% of ads, used on 55.0% of chain ads and 26.4% of independent ads.

## <span className="he">💬</span> How ads open

We also classified the opening angle of each ad with Jev, a classification model from TypeSafe.

A result or transformation, such as smoother skin or fuller lips, opened 35.4% of ads.

A price or deal opened 21.6%, rising to 29.3% for independents against 17.1% for chains.

A problem or concern, such as shaving or fine lines, opened 12.3%.

Reviews and social proof opened 5.1% of ads, and provider credentials opened 3.5%.

## <span className="he">🧭</span> What this means for practice owners

The data describes what med spas run, not what works, so read it as a map of the market rather than a playbook.

Two things stand out for an independent practice.

First, price is the crowded lane.

Nearly half of independent ads show a price, and Botox specials cluster tightly between $8.50 and $11 a unit, so a price alone rarely stands apart from the spa down the road.

Second, many independent ads end somewhere other than a booking page.

When an ad sends people to a chat or an Instagram profile, the booking depends on someone answering quickly, and that step is where interest often gets lost.

If you're reviewing your own ads, start with the copy: the [free med spa ad checker](/medspa-ad-checker/) flags wording that Meta and regulators scrutinize, and our [ad claims study](/blog/med-spa-ad-claims-study/) shows how often other spas use it.

## <span className="he">🔬</span> How we did it

**Source.** Ads came from Meta's public Ad Library, filtered to ads shown in the United States, collected on October 4, 2026.

**Sample.** We pulled every ad from 14 national aesthetic brands and ran keyword searches for "med spa", "lip filler" and "botox special". Ads from the "botox special" search were used only for the per-unit Botox price, because a search for a special returns price ads by design.

**Cleaning.** We removed advertisers that aren't U.S. aesthetic providers: product brands, media, influencers promoting products, training courses, clinics outside the U.S. and wellness-only clinics. We also removed catalog ads whose copy is generated by Meta and not visible in the library. Each unique piece of ad copy counts once, which left 570 ads from 184 advertisers.

**Chains.** 16 brands with locations in several states count as national chains. Ads run by an individual injector's page that send clicks to a chain's own website count as that chain. Chain figures are weighted toward the brands that run the most ads: the three largest supplied 54.4% of chain ads.

**Measures.** Prices, percent-off offers, "free" and deadline wording were found with plain text rules that anyone can repeat on the same ads. Treatment, offer type and opening angle were classified by Jev. Run length is measured from the ad's start date to its end date, or to October 4 for ads still running.

**What it can't see.** The Ad Library doesn't show spend, reach, clicks or bookings, so nothing here measures performance. Text inside images and video wasn't read. Keyword searches return a sample of matching ads, not every med spa ad in the country, so independent figures describe independents who advertise with those words.

**Data.** The aggregate figures are available as a [CSV download](/data/med-spa-advertising-2026.csv). We don't publish business names or individual ads.
