Most plastic surgery practices try to grow the same way: more ads, a new agency, a bigger top of the funnel.

Sometimes that works.

More often the new inquiries leak out at the same places the old ones did, and the practice ends up paying more for the same number of surgeries.

Growing a practice is less about finding new patients than about losing fewer of the ones who already found you, and then adding demand in the right order.

## <span className="he">🧮</span> The growth equation

Every surgical practice's revenue comes from the same four numbers.

<Equation title="Monthly surgical revenue" result="Revenue" caption="Each term is a separate lever with its own fixes. Improve any one and revenue rises; improve all four and the gains multiply.">
<Term label="Inquiries" note="Demand" />
<Term label="Consult rate" note="Follow-up" />
<Term label="Surgery rate" note="The consult" />
<Term label="Case value" note="Per patient" />
</Equation>

Inquiries are demand: how many people contact you.

Consult rate is how many of those inquiries book a consultation, which mostly comes down to follow-up speed and the coordinator.

Surgery rate is how many consultations become surgery, which depends on the consult experience, pricing clarity, and financing.

Case value is what each surgical patient is worth, including combination procedures and the patients who come back.

The [consultation conversion benchmarks](/blog/plastic-surgery-consultation-conversion-rate/) give typical ranges for each rate.

## <span className="he">📈</span> Why small gains everywhere beat one big push

Because the terms multiply, a modest improvement at every stage does more than a bigger improvement at one.

Here's an illustrative practice: 2,000 website visitors a month, a 2 percent inquiry rate, 40 percent of inquiries booking a consult, 40 percent of consults becoming surgery, and a $10,000 average case.

That's about 6.4 surgeries and $64,000 a month.

Ten percent more traffic adds about $6,400 a month, and it usually costs money every month to keep.

Ten percent better at each of the three conversion rates adds about $21,200, from the same visitors.

That's the core of how to grow a plastic surgery practice: work the whole equation, not just the first term.

## <span className="he">🧭</span> Which lever to pull first

The levers differ in two ways that matter: how fast they show results, and how much ongoing spend they need.

Start in the shaded corner.

Follow-up speed, website conversion, and the consult itself can move within a month or two, and they make every inquiry you already pay for worth more.

Next, start the compounding levers, local SEO and reviews, because they take months and the clock only starts when you do.

Add paid ads last, or scale them last, because paid traffic sent into a leaky funnel just leaks faster.

## <span className="he">🔎</span> How to attract new plastic surgery patients

When the funnel converts, new patients are worth chasing, and they research in predictable places.

- **Google, locally.** The map pack and procedure-plus-city searches, won with a strong [Google Business Profile](/blog/plastic-surgery-google-business-profile/) and procedure pages.
- **Instagram.** Results and the surgeon's personality, covered in [plastic surgeon Instagram marketing](/blog/plastic-surgeon-instagram/).
- **Review sites.** Many patients check [RealSelf and Google reviews](/blog/plastic-surgery-reviews-realself/) before they ever inquire.
- **Referrals.** Past patients and other providers, still the highest-intent source most practices have.

Wherever they come from, they land on your website and your phone line, so that's where the conversion work pays off twice.

## <span className="he">⚖️</span> When growth means value, not volume

Growth has a ceiling most marketing advice ignores: the surgeon's calendar.

<Compare>

<Side title="Calendar has open time" tone="yes">

- Grow volume: more inquiries and higher conversion
- Fill slow months with the off-season start
- Paid demand can make sense

</Side>

<Side title="Calendar is already full" tone="neutral">

- Grow case value, not case count
- Favor the procedures worth the most to you
- Raise prices before adding surgeries you can't schedule

</Side>

</Compare>

A practice booked months out doesn't need more inquiries.

It needs better ones, and a pricing and procedure mix that makes each surgery worth more.

## <span className="he">📝</span> A one-page growth plan

A plastic surgery business plan doesn't need fifty pages of marketing.

It needs five steps you'll actually follow.

<Steps>

<Step title="Measure the chain">

Pull last quarter's visitors, inquiries, consults, surgeries, and average case value. If you can't get one of them, that's your first fix.

</Step>

<Step title="Fix the biggest leak">

Put a dollar value on each stage's gap to the benchmark and fix the largest first, one change at a time.

</Step>

<Step title="Start a compounding channel">

Local SEO and a weekly review routine, started now, because they take months to pay off.

</Step>

<Step title="Add paid speed">

Once the funnel converts, add ads and judge them by cost per booked surgery, not cost per lead.

</Step>

<Step title="Review monthly">

Same five numbers, same day each month, judged on inquiry cohorts old enough to have matured.

</Step>

</Steps>
