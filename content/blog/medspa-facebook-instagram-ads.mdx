Med spa ads work when three things line up: the right channel, an offer worth clicking, and a landing page built to turn that click into a booked consult.

This guide covers all three for Facebook and Instagram, plus the formats, the health ad rules, the tracking, the budget, and the metrics that tell you whether your advertising is growing the business.

It is written for med spa owners who run their own ads or want to judge the people running them.

## <span className="he">⚖️</span> Google PPC vs Meta: where med spa ads should run

Google Ads is pay-per-click (PPC) search advertising: you pay every time someone clicks your ad after searching for something like "lip filler near me."

That sounds ideal, because the person is already looking.

The catch, in our experience, is price: clicks on treatment searches are very expensive, and the med spas near you are bidding on the same short list of keywords.

Facebook and Instagram work the other way around.

Nobody opens Instagram to buy Botox, so a Meta ad's job is to create demand by putting a compelling offer in front of local people who weren't searching yet.

<Compare>

<Side title="Google Ads (PPC)" tone="neutral">

- Catches people who are already searching
- You pay per click, and treatment clicks are expensive
- Every local competitor bids on the same keywords

</Side>

<Side title="Facebook and Instagram" tone="yes">

- Reaches local people before they start searching
- Visual formats that show the treatment and the offer
- One ad account covers both platforms

</Side>

</Compare>

Our view, from running campaigns: for most med spas, Meta ads get better results for the money than Google, which is why the ads we manage run on Meta instead.

That is an opinion from experience, not a law of nature, so if you want to test paid search, the [med spa Google Ads guide](/blog/medspa-google-ads/) covers how to run it without wasting budget.

The same logic applies to organic social media posting.

Posting on your own social media feed is slow to produce new clients and may not work at all, so we tell practice owners to put that effort into paid ads, where a budget buys reach on day one.

## <span className="he">📍</span> How Facebook and Instagram advertising reaches local clients

Meta runs Facebook and Instagram from one ad account, so a single campaign can show your ad in the Facebook feed, the Instagram feed, Stories, and Reels.

You target by location first: a radius around your practice, sized to how far clients will actually drive for a treatment.

What you can't do is target by health.

Meta has removed detailed targeting options tied to sensitive topics like health conditions, so you can't aim a medical spa ad at people based on a condition or a treatment they seem interested in.

That makes the creative your real targeting: a sharp offer for dermal fillers finds the people who want dermal fillers, because they are the ones who stop and click.

<Callout type="key">

On Meta, the creative is your targeting.

A scroll-stopping visual with a clear offer will outperform any amount of audience tweaking.

</Callout>

## <span className="he">🧩</span> Med spa ad formats on Facebook and Instagram

Meta offers several ad formats, and each suits a different job.

<Cards>

<Card icon="page" title="Single image">

One static image, one message, one offer. Fast to make, fast to test, and hard to beat for a clear promotion.

</Card>

<Card icon="link" title="Carousel">

Several swipeable cards in one ad. Useful for a treatment menu, a step-by-step, or several angles on one offer.

</Card>

<Card icon="zap" title="Video and Reels">

Short vertical video in feeds, Stories, and Reels. Strong when trust is the barrier, but it needs real footage from the practice.

</Card>

<Card icon="target" title="Instant lead form">

A pre-filled form that opens inside Facebook or Instagram. Cheap leads, lower intent, as the [lead forms vs landing pages](/blog/facebook-lead-forms-vs-landing-pages/) comparison explains.

</Card>

<Card icon="star" title="Collection">

A cover image or video with product tiles underneath. Built for retail catalogs, so rarely the best fit for a treatment menu.

</Card>

<Card icon="calendar" title="Offer-led ads">

A first-visit price or a limited-time promotion is the message, not a separate ad type. It works inside any format above.

</Card>

</Cards>

The ads we manage are static images, 8–12 fresh ones a month, because volume of good tests matters more than production value.

A practice that wants Reels needs its own footage, and the [ad creative guide](/blog/medspa-ad-creative/) covers when static beats video and when video wins.

## <span className="he">🎨</span> Creative, content and offers that get the click

The biggest lever in any med spa ad is the offer.

A specific, believable offer aimed at a real objection beats a vague "book your consultation today" in any format.

First-visit offers and limited-time promotions are the usual starting point, and the [intro offers guide](/blog/medspa-intro-offers/) covers how to structure one without training clients to wait for discounts.

<Checklist>

- Lead with the outcome the client wants, not the product name.
- Use real faces and your real practice instead of stock imagery.
- Put one specific offer in the ad, with a reason to act now.
- Make the next step obvious, because a beautiful ad with no clear action just entertains people.

</Checklist>

The second lever is the hook: the image or first line that decides whether anyone reads the rest.

Production value is a distant third.

## <span className="he">🎯</span> Send every ad to a treatment-specific landing page

This is where most med spa marketing on Meta breaks.

The ad does its job, the click arrives, and it lands on the practice website's homepage, with a navigation bar, a dozen treatments, and plenty of exits.

A cold click from a Meta ad needs the opposite: one treatment, one offer, one form.

That is what a procedure-specific landing page is for, and the [med spa landing pages guide](/blog/medspa-landing-pages/) walks through how to build one.

Meta traffic is cooler than search traffic, so it needs a stronger landing experience to become a consult.

## <span className="he">🛑</span> Health ad rules: injectables, before-and-afters and LegitScript

Ads for medical spa services sit in one of Meta's most restricted categories, and the rules shape both creative and targeting.

<Callout type="warning">

Meta's advertising standards bar before-and-after images in ads for personal health and appearance, and ads for cosmetic procedures must be targeted to people 18 and older.

Build your creative around lifestyle, education, and offers from the start, not after a rejection.

</Callout>

Brand-name injectables are the other trap.

Botox, Dysport and Juvéderm are prescription products, and both Meta and Google restrict ads that promote prescription drugs.

In some cases the platforms require LegitScript certification, the third-party healthcare verification they use, before those ads can run at all.

The practical approach is to advertise the outcome and the offer, then let the landing page explain the treatment.

Meta also limits how some health and wellness advertisers can use conversion events for optimization, which changes how campaigns should be set up.

Read the [healthcare ad policy guide](/blog/medspa-healthcare-ad-policy/) and the broader [med spa advertising laws](/blog/medspa-marketing-laws/) before you launch, and check current policy, because the rules change.

## <span className="he">📡</span> Tracking: the pixel, UTMs, iOS and HIPAA

Meta needs conversion data to know who to show your ads to, and you need it to know what your marketing is worth.

The Meta pixel and the Conversions API send conversion events from your landing page back to Meta.

Since Apple's iOS 14 privacy changes, iPhone users who opt out of tracking are invisible to the pixel, so Meta's own reports undercount results.

That is why every ad should carry UTM parameters, so your own records show which ad and which campaign each lead came from.

<Callout type="warning">

Tracking pixels can leak patient information to ad platforms if they fire on the wrong pages, like a treatment-specific booking confirmation.

Read [HIPAA-aware website tracking](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/) before you install a pixel, and keep conversion events limited to non-sensitive steps.

</Callout>

On the campaigns we manage, conversions are tracked on what we build: the landing pages and the forms.

We connect a practice's online booking software for conversion tracking only when it is needed.

For phone bookings and patients who find you three different ways, the [attribution and call tracking guide](/blog/medspa-attribution-call-tracking/) covers the rest.

## <span className="he">📊</span> The ad metrics that matter

Meta reports dozens of numbers, and only a handful tell you whether your ads are building the business.

| Metric | What it tells you | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| CTR (click-through rate) | Share of people who saw the ad and clicked | Is the creative and offer stopping the scroll? |
| CPC (cost per click) | What each click costs | Rising CPC on the same ad often means fatigue |
| Conversion rate | Share of landing page visitors who become leads | The page's job, and the cheapest number to improve |
| CPL (cost per lead) | Ad spend divided by leads | Cheap leads that never book are not cheap |
| CPA (cost per acquisition) | Ad spend divided by booked consults or patients | The number to manage the budget by |
| ROAS (return on ad spend) | Revenue from ad-sourced patients divided by spend | Count repeat visits, not just the first one |

The trap is cost per lead.

A lead only matters if it books, so the number to watch is cost per booked consult.

## <span className="he">💵</span> How much should a med spa budget for ads?

There is no honest universal number, so set your budget with a method instead of a rule of thumb.

Start with what a new client is worth: the value of their first visit plus the visits that follow, which the [patient lifetime value guide](/blog/medspa-patient-lifetime-value/) shows how to calculate.

Then decide the most you would pay for one booked consult and still be happy.

Your monthly budget is that target cost per booked consult times the number of new consults you want, adjusted once real numbers come in.

Budget enough for Meta to learn, too, because a campaign starved of conversions never gets out of guesswork.

For the managed Meta ads we run, the ad spend minimum is $3,000 a month, paid by the practice directly to Meta and never marked up, for exactly that reason.

The management itself is a flat $3,000 a month after a $2,500 one-time setup, never a percentage of spend, and the [managed ads page](/medspa-advertising/) lays out what that includes.

## <span className="he">🔁</span> Managing campaigns: testing, fresh creative and ad fatigue

Med spa ads don't fail all at once; they wear out.

The same local audience sees the same ad for weeks, frequency climbs, the cost per result creeps up, and the owner decides Facebook stopped working.

The fix is a steady routine, not a rescue.

<Checklist>

- Ship fresh creative every month so no ad runs long enough to go stale.
- Test one variable at a time: the offer, the hook, or the image.
- Move budget to the winners and cut the losers early.
- Run one landing-page A/B test a month, because the page improves the value of every click.
- Retarget people who visited but didn't book.

</Checklist>

[Retargeting](/blog/medspa-retargeting/) is where Meta often earns its keep, because showing your offer to people who already visited converts better than reaching strangers.

The [A/B testing guide](/blog/ab-testing-medspa/) covers how to run page tests that actually tell you something.

## <span className="he">⏳</span> How long before med spa ads show ROI?

Nobody can honestly promise a timeline, because it depends on your market, your offer, your budget, and how fast your team follows up.

A few things are predictable.

Meta says a new ad set generally needs around 50 optimization events in a week to exit its learning phase, and results are noisy until it does.

Tests need enough traffic to say anything, so the first month is mostly about learning which offer and which page work.

And the return on a new client builds over repeat visits, so judging ads on the first appointment alone understates them.

That is why the managed program here has a 3-month minimum: shorter than that, you are judging a campaign before it has had a fair chance.
