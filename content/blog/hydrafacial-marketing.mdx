## <span className="he">🧴</span> What the treatment actually is (for your ad copy)

Patients book what they understand, so your pages and ads should describe the treatment in plain language.

Hydrafacial's own [treatment page](https://www.hydrafacial.com/pages/the-treatment) lists seven steps, powered by what it calls patented Vortex-Fusion technology:

- **Hydrate:** nourishing serums to boost hydration.
- **Microdermabrasion:** exfoliation with specialized tips.
- **Peel:** a customizable, no-downtime chemical peel.
- **Extract:** clears pores, which Hydrafacial says happens without discomfort or visible redness.
- **Booster:** personalized serums that target specific skin concerns.
- **LED:** blue-light and red-light therapy.
- **Lymphatic drainage:** for a depuffed, more lifted look.

The same page describes tiers, from a foundational treatment up to versions that add boosters, LED light therapy, and lymphatic drainage.

Stick to the manufacturer's wording for any benefit claim, and let your esthetician explain the rest in the room.

Boosters and add-ons raise the ticket per visit.

Hydrafacial describes boosters as targeted, professional-grade serums that personalize the treatment, which makes them an easy, natural upgrade rather than a hard sell.

Brief booster education on your treatment page and at checkout, plus member pricing on add-ons, lifts ticket value without discounting the base treatment.

The third multiplier is the next treatment.

A HydraFacial client already trusts your team with their skin, so they're the warmest audience you have for [Botox and injectables](/blog/botox-marketing/), [chemical peels](/blog/chemical-peel-marketing/), and laser treatments.

### How to structure a HydraFacial membership

Keep it simple: one treatment a month included, member pricing on boosters and add-ons, and clear rules for skipped months and cancellation.

Price it from your own treatment cost, provider time, and target margin, not from what the spa down the street charges.

Model the worst case, where every member uses every benefit, before you launch.

### Should you discount HydraFacial?

A first-visit offer can bring in new clients, but a standing discount trains them to wait for the next deal.

Tie any intro offer to the second visit, like a credit toward the first month of membership, and read the [pricing strategy guide](/blog/medspa-pricing-strategy/) before you cut the price.

## <span className="he">📣</span> Paid ads: Meta over Google for HydraFacial

HydraFacial is one of the easier med spa treatments to advertise, because it isn't a prescription product and avoids the drug-ad rules that apply to injectables.

It still falls under Meta's [Health and Wellness ad standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/): cosmetic procedure ads must target people 18 and older, and they can't contain statements of inferiority that attack the viewer's appearance.

Before-and-after images are allowed under the current version, with written patient consent, but Meta has revised this rule more than once, so the [Meta health and wellness ad policy guide](/blog/medspa-healthcare-ad-policy/) is worth reading before launch.

## <span className="he">🎥</span> Content your team can create

What social media content performs best for HydraFacial?

In our experience, the posts that work as ads show the treatment itself, not stock beauty shots.

Posting organically is slow to produce new clients and may not work at all, so put that effort into creative for paid ads.

<Checklist>

- Film the extraction step (Hydrafacial's vortex extraction at work) as short vertical video.
- Show the post-treatment glow on real clients, with written consent.
- Have your esthetician explain boosters in 20 seconds.
- Show what a membership includes, in plain language.

</Checklist>

This is content your practice films in-house, and the [before-and-after photos guide](/blog/medspa-before-after-photos/) covers consent and quality.

Before-and-afters can run in Meta ads under the current rules (18+ targeting, no statements of inferiority), and any client testimonial must follow the [FTC endorsement rules](/blog/ftc-testimonial-rules-medspa/).
