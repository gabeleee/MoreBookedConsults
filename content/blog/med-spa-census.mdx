## <span className="he">📊</span> The headline numbers

We pulled every open Google Maps listing in the United States whose main category is "Medical spa."

There were 31,689 of them in September 2026.

That's roughly three times the 9,500 med spas that industry estimates usually cite.

The two numbers measure different things.

A business chooses its own Google category, so the listings include every location of a multi-site company, day spas and wellness clinics that offer a few medical treatments, and injectors working out of a salon suite.

For a practice owner, the listing count is the more useful number, because it's the competition a patient sees when they search "med spa near me."

## <span className="he">💻</span> Websites and advertising

1 in 10 med spa listings (10.6%) has no website at all.

Another 4.9% link to a booking page, an Instagram profile or a link-in-bio page instead of a site of their own.

We then read the homepage code of the 23,954 own websites our crawler could reach.

- 13.4% carry a Meta pixel, the tag used to track and target Facebook and Instagram ads.
- 10.2% carry a Google Ads tag.
- 20.1% carry at least one of the two.

Chains are more than twice as likely to have a Meta pixel as single-location med spas (25.4% against 11.2%).

These are minimums.

A tag loaded later by a tag manager doesn't always show in the homepage code, so some sites that advertise won't be counted.

Social media is close to universal.

74.4% of med spa websites link to an Instagram account, 65.1% to Facebook and 17.0% to TikTok.

## <span className="he">⭐</span> Reviews

The typical med spa listing has a 5.0-star rating and 30 Google reviews.

31.2% of listings have fewer than 10 reviews.

With ratings bunched at the top, the review count, not the stars, is what separates one listing from the next in a crowded market.

## <span className="he">🧭</span> What this means for practice owners

Your real competition is the listing count, not the industry estimate.

In Arizona, Colorado or Florida, a patient searching nearby sees close to twice as many options per person as the national average.

Most of your competitors are single-location businesses like you, and 4 in 5 of them show no sign of paid ad tracking on their website.

That leaves room for the practices that measure their ads and their booking flow.

## <span className="he">🔬</span> How we did it

**Listings.** We pulled every U.S. Google Maps business listing in the medical spa category through DataForSEO's Business Listings API in September 2026. We kept open listings whose main category is "Medical spa," which removed closed businesses and listings whose main category is something else, such as plastic surgeon or dermatologist.

**Population.** Per-person figures use the U.S. Census Bureau's Vintage 2024 state population estimates. Five territories and listings without a state were left out of the state figures.

**Ownership.** Listings were grouped by the domain of their website. Booking platforms, social profiles and link-in-bio pages were not treated as a website, so independent med spas that share a booking platform aren't counted as a chain.

**Websites.** A crawler that respected robots.txt fetched each own website's homepage and contact page and looked for a Meta pixel, a Google Ads tag and links to social profiles.

**What it can't see.** A Google listing isn't a license, and the category is chosen by the business. One business can have several listings, and the tag figures are minimums.

**Data.** The state table is available as a [CSV download](/data/med-spa-census-2026-states.csv). We don't publish business names.
