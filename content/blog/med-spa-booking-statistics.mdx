## <span className="he">📊</span> The headline numbers

We drew a random sample of 600 U.S. med spa websites and opened each homepage the way a patient on a phone would see it.

509 of them were live business sites we could measure, and the rest were down, blocked automated visits, or turned out to be parked or placeholder pages.

## <span className="he">📅</span> How many med spas offer online booking

68.6% of med spa websites let a visitor reach an online booking system within three taps of the homepage (October 2026, 509 sites).

The other 31.4% didn't, which usually meant the Book button led to a contact form, an appointment request form or a phone number.

A booking request form isn't online booking, because the patient still has to wait for someone to call back and offer a time.

Among sites that did offer online booking, the median path was a single tap.

For 5.4% of the sites with online booking, the shortest path ran through a link inside a closed menu, which costs the visitor an extra tap.

No site in the sample needed three taps, so a booking path is usually either short or missing entirely.

## <span className="he">📱</span> The first screen on a phone

65.4% of med spa homepages showed a book, schedule or appointment button on the first screen of an iPhone-sized display, without scrolling or opening a menu.

85.7% showed one somewhere on the homepage without opening a menu.

34.2% put a tap-to-call link on the first screen, and 72.5% had one somewhere on the homepage.

26.7% of homepages showed neither a book button nor a tap-to-call link on the first screen, so a visitor ready to act had to scroll or open the menu to find a next step.

Only 5.5% had a text-us (sms:) link on the homepage.

The online booking gap of 16.2 points is well outside the margin of error, with 95% confidence intervals of 72.8% to 85.3% and 58.4% to 68.4%.

Sites with a Meta pixel or Google Ads tag, a sign the practice advertises, offered online booking 73.1% of the time, against 66.5% for sites without one.

That gap is within the margin of error at these sample sizes (160 and 349 sites), so we don't read it as a real difference.

Advertisers were more likely to show a book button on the first screen, 73.8% against 61.6%.

## <span className="he">💬</span> Chat and contact forms

13.4% of homepages ran a chat or text widget, most often a HighLevel chat bubble, Podium or Growth99.

We found a contact form with three or more visible fields on the contact page of 240 sites.

The median form had five fields, and 13.8% had seven or more.

Only 17.5% of those forms asked which treatment the person was interested in, and 5.4% asked when they'd like to come in.

A form that skips the treatment question leaves the front desk to start every follow-up call from zero.

## <span className="he">⚡</span> Mobile basics are mostly solved

Almost every site passed the basic mobile checks.

All 509 homepages had a mobile viewport tag, and 0.6% were wider than the phone screen, forcing sideways scrolling.

The median homepage finished loading in 1.4 seconds in our test, and 2.6% took five seconds or more.

Load times come from a single visit per site from one location, so treat them as a rough guide rather than a speed test.

## <span className="he">🧭</span> What this means for practice owners

The data describes how med spa websites are built, not how many bookings they get, so read it as a benchmark rather than a scorecard.

Three things stand out.

First, nearly a third of med spa websites still don't offer online booking a patient can finish alone.

Every "book" button that opens a request form adds a wait, and the patient may book somewhere else before the call back comes, which is why [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-medspa/) matters so much for those practices.

Second, the first phone screen is often wasted.

More than a quarter of homepages gave a ready-to-book visitor no button and no phone link without scrolling.

Third, smaller practices trail on every booking measure we checked, which also makes these some of the easiest gaps for a smaller med spa to close.

## <span className="he">🔬</span> How we did it

**Population.** 22,646 U.S. Google Maps listings with the primary category "Medical spa" and a website on the business's own domain, one per domain, from the listing data behind our [US med spa census](/blog/med-spa-census/). Listings whose website was a booking page, social profile or link-in-bio page were excluded.

**Sample.** A seeded random sample of 600 domains. 49 were down, 20 blocked automated visits, 3 disallowed crawling in robots.txt, 2 failed to load or redirected to a booking platform, and 17 were parked, for sale, host placeholder or "we have moved" pages. That left 509 live med spa websites.

**Visit.** Each homepage was loaded once on October 15, 2026, in a headless Chrome browser set to an iPhone screen (390 × 844 pixels), and checked again after scrolling and waiting for late-loading widgets. We followed robots.txt and never bypassed a block.

**Online booking.** A site counts as having online booking when a visitor can reach a scheduling system from the homepage: a link to a booking platform, a booking widget opened by a Book button, or a page with a booking system embedded. We followed booking links up to three taps deep, and a link reachable only through a closed menu counts one extra tap. We recognize 39 booking systems by their web addresses and scripts, plus schedulers on other domains that a Book button links to. A booking system we couldn't recognize counts as no online booking, so the 31.4% figure is an upper bound.

**Checked against real audits.** Before the run, we tested the method on the sites behind our own booking audits, where the booking path had been checked by hand, and it matched on every site it could reach. We then hand-checked a random set of "no online booking" sites and fixed the rules that had missed a booking system before computing these figures.

**What it can't see.** We didn't submit forms, make bookings or send messages, so nothing here measures response time, availability or how many visitors book. Forms inside embedded frames weren't read. Results describe one visit per site on one day.

**Data.** The aggregate figures, with sample sizes and 95% confidence intervals, are available as a [CSV download](/data/med-spa-booking-benchmarks-2026.csv). We don't publish business names or individual site results.
