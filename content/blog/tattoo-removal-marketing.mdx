Tattoo removal is one of the higher-intent treatments a med spa can market, because almost nobody searches for it casually.

That intent is an asset, but it comes with a responsibility: to set honest expectations about a long, patient process.

## <span className="he">📈</span> Demand and seasonality

Tattoo removal demand comes down to a simple profile.

## <span className="he">💰</span> Offer economics: a long series

<Callout type="key">

Full removal typically takes many sessions over many months, so tattoo removal is inherently a long series, and pricing should reflect that.

Package or per-session-with-a-plan structures fit the reality and add up to significant revenue per committed patient.

</Callout>

The honest [intro-to-plan](/blog/medspa-intro-offers/) framing works well: a first session that begins a clearly explained multi-visit journey.

## <span className="he">⚖️</span> Ad-policy constraints

Tattoo removal avoids the prescription-drug rules that complicate Botox ads, but it isn't free of restrictions.

Meta's [Health and Wellness ad standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/) still treat it as a cosmetic procedure.

- **Target 18 and older** for any cosmetic procedure ad.
- **No statements of inferiority,** so copy can't attack the viewer's appearance or past choices.
- **No timeframe promises without qualifiers,** such as full removal in a set number of sessions.

Meta can also restrict the data your pixel sends back if it classifies your site as health-related, which the [Meta health and wellness ad policy guide](/blog/medspa-healthcare-ad-policy/) explains.

In our experience Google clicks for "tattoo removal near me" searches are very expensive, and Meta ads get med spas better results, so Meta is what we run, as our [med spa advertising](/medspa-advertising/) page explains.

Keep claims about complete removal and timelines honest in every channel, since results vary by ink and skin.
