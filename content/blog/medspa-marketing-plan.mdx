A med spa marketing plan isn't a list of tactics, it's an order of operations.

Getting the order right is what separates practices that build momentum from ones that spin.

Most marketing advice for med spas hands you twenty channels at once.

This roadmap does the opposite: it front-loads the foundations, so every later dollar works harder, and it tells you what to leave for later.

It's written for a medical spa that is already open and seeing patients.

If you're still pre-opening, start with [how to market a new med spa](/blog/how-to-market-new-medspa/) and the [pre-opening waitlist guide](/blog/medspa-pre-opening-marketing/), and put the marketing section of your [business plan](/blog/medspa-business-plan-marketing/) together there.

## <span className="he">🧭</span> Before month one: who your med spa serves, and why they'd pick you

A plan needs a target before it needs tactics.

The [industry statistics](/blog/medspa-industry-statistics/) show a growing market, and more practices chasing the same local clients.

That makes positioning the first line of the plan, not a nice-to-have.

Write down three things on one page:

- **Who you want more of.** The patients and treatments that are worth the most to your business, like injectable regulars, laser packages, or skin programs.
- **Why they'd choose you.** Your USP in one sentence: the injector's experience, a specialty, a result you're known for, convenience, or price. If a competitor down the street could say the same sentence, keep going.
- **How you sound.** A consistent name, look, and voice across your site, profile, and ads. The [branding and naming guide](/blog/medspa-branding-naming/) covers the basics.

Every later decision, from which treatment pages to build to which offer goes in an ad, comes back to this page.

## <span className="he">🧮</span> Find the leak before you plan the spend

Many med spas lose patients between the first inquiry and the treatment chair, not just at the top of the funnel.

So the plan starts by measuring the chain.

Pull last quarter's numbers for each term.

If inquiries are healthy but few of them book, the problem is follow-up speed or the booking flow, and [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-medspa/) is the first fix.

If bookings are healthy but patients don't show, work on reminders and deposits, covered in the [no-shows guide](/blog/medspa-no-shows-consult-conversion/).

If you can't get one of the numbers at all, that's the first item in month one.

## <span className="he">🧱</span> Months 1-2: build the med spa marketing foundation

<Callout type="key">

Before you spend a dollar on ads, get the fundamentals right, because they multiply everything that follows.

Skip them and you're pouring water into a leaky bucket.

</Callout>

This phase feels slow because it isn't flashy, but it's the highest-leverage work you'll do all year.

**Your website converts:** many of your visitors are on a phone, so the site has to load fast and make booking obvious with a thumb.

The [speed and mobile guide](/blog/medspa-website-speed-mobile/) covers the technical side.

Give each core treatment its own page, and send paid traffic to [landing pages](/blog/medspa-landing-pages/) built for one offer, with a short [multi-step form](/blog/medspa-multi-step-forms/) instead of a long one.

Converting the traffic you already have is what [med spa CRO](/medspa-cro/) is for.

**Your Google Business Profile is complete:** pick the most accurate primary category, list every treatment as a service, and add fresh photos.

Clean up your [citations](/blog/medspa-citations-directories/) so your name, address, and phone read the same on every directory.

**Your tracking works:** set up Google Analytics (GA4) with a conversion event for every form, call, and booking, so you can see which source each inquiry came from.

The [attribution and call tracking guide](/blog/medspa-attribution-call-tracking/) shows how.

Tracking pixels can leak patient information to ad platforms, so read the [HIPAA website tracking guide](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/) before you add one.

**A review habit starts:** ask every patient at checkout or in a follow-up message, make the link one tap, and reply to every review.

Don't reward patients for reviews or filter for happy ones, and see [how to get more reviews](/blog/medspa-get-more-reviews/) for scripts.

**A before-and-after library starts:** get specific written consent, and shoot with the same lighting and angles every time.

The [before-and-after photos guide](/blog/medspa-before-after-photos/) covers consent and the rules on where you can use them.

<Checklist>

- Site loads fast on a phone and has a booking path above the fold
- A page for each core treatment, and a landing page for each paid offer
- Google Business Profile complete, with every treatment listed
- Name, address, and phone identical across directories
- GA4 conversion events on every form, call, and booking
- A review ask built into checkout
- A consented before-and-after library, growing weekly

</Checklist>

## <span className="he">📣</span> Months 3-4: turn on demand

With the foundation solid, start driving traffic that now has somewhere good to land.

<Cards>

<Card icon="pin" title="Local SEO">

[Local SEO](/medspa-seo/) is the owned channel that compounds. Start with [keyword research](/blog/medspa-keyword-research/) for treatment-plus-city searches, then work toward the [Google map pack](/blog/how-to-rank-medspa-google-maps/).

</Card>

<Card icon="page" title="Content">

One helpful page at a time, answering the questions patients search before they book. Planned around a topical map, [content marketing](/blog/medspa-content-marketing/) feeds your local SEO.

</Card>

<Card icon="target" title="Meta ads">

[Facebook and Instagram ads](/blog/medspa-facebook-instagram-ads/) put a specific offer in front of nearby people, then send them to a landing page built to book.

</Card>

<Card icon="star" title="Referrals">

[Referral programs](/blog/medspa-referral-programs/) turn happy clients into your cheapest channel. Reward both sides with a treatment credit, and check your state's rules first.

</Card>

</Cards>

Because your site converts and your tracking works, you can see what's paying off instead of guessing.

Local SEO takes months to compound, and the clock only starts when you do, so start it here even though paid ads will show results sooner.

A nice side effect of building topical authority is that AI search tools can start to find and cite your pages too.

### Google Ads or Meta ads for med spas?

Here's our view, from experience rather than a benchmark.

Google Ads clicks for aesthetic treatments are very expensive, and Meta ads get med spas better results for the money.

That's why the ads we run for clients are on Meta, with fresh static image creative every month sent to landing pages we build and test.

One Meta campaign runs on both Facebook and Instagram, and Instagram's visual format suits aesthetic offers.

If you want to test search anyway, the [Google Ads guide](/blog/medspa-google-ads/) shows how to do it without wasting budget.

Either way, check the [healthcare ad policies](/blog/medspa-healthcare-ad-policy/) first, because before-and-after images and health claims carry real restrictions on both platforms.

If you'd rather have Meta ads run for you, see [managed med spa Facebook ads](/medspa-facebook-ads/).

### Where social media, influencers, and patient content fit

Organic social media is on every plan template, so here's our honest take.

In our experience posting is slow to produce bookings, and for many practices it never books many at all.

We tell clients to put that time and money into paid ads instead, and to treat the feed as proof for people who find them elsewhere.

If you do post, keep it to the content patients trust most: consented before-and-afters, an injector explaining a treatment, and answers to consult questions.

User-generated content and testimonials follow the same rule: get permission in writing, and keep them honest, per the [FTC testimonial rules](/blog/ftc-testimonial-rules-medspa/).

[Influencer marketing](/blog/medspa-influencer-marketing/) works best with local creators, a written agreement, a trackable code, and clear disclosure of any free treatment or payment.

## <span className="he">📈</span> Months 5-6: optimize and retain clients

Now you refine, and you make patients stick.

<Checklist>

- Double down on the channels your data shows are working.
- Cut what isn't.
- Test one change at a time on your best landing page, and keep the winner.
- Shift focus to [retention](/blog/medspa-client-retention/): [memberships](/blog/medspa-membership-programs/), a rebooking habit, and win-backs that turn first visits into lasting revenue.
- Offer existing clients the treatment that naturally pairs with the one they already get.

</Checklist>

**Email and SMS carry most of the retention work:** a welcome series, rebooking reminders timed to each treatment's cycle, and win-backs for lapsed patients.

The [email marketing](/blog/medspa-email-marketing/) and [SMS marketing](/blog/medspa-sms-marketing/) guides cover what to send and how often.

Most of it runs from your booking software, so you can set it up in-house.

**Bundling and upselling raise the value of every client you already have:** pair treatments that belong together, like a neurotoxin client and a skin program, into a package or a membership tier.

The [pricing strategy guide](/blog/medspa-pricing-strategy/) covers how to package without training clients to wait for discounts.

Retention is what makes month six worth more than month one, because you're no longer starting from zero each month.

## <span className="he">📊</span> Med spa KPIs: how you'll know the plan is working

A plan without numbers is a wish list.

Track a short list, review it on the same day each month, and let it decide what you scale and what you cut.

<Cards>

<Card icon="funnel" title="Booking rate">

Whether inquiries become booked consults. Forms and calls vs consults on the calendar.

</Card>

<Card icon="calendar" title="Show rate">

Whether booked patients actually attend. It lives in your booking software.

</Card>

<Card icon="target" title="Cost per booked consult">

What each channel really costs: spend divided by booked consults, per source.

</Card>

<Card icon="zap" title="Click-through rate (CTR)">

Whether an ad's creative earns the click. Useful for creative, never the final verdict.

</Card>

<Card icon="chart" title="Lifetime value vs acquisition cost">

Whether a new client is profitable over time, not just on the first visit.

</Card>

<Card icon="check" title="Rebooking rate">

Whether first visits turn into regulars, the number retention work moves.

</Card>

</Cards>

Return on investment (ROI) comes down to one comparison: the revenue from clients a channel brought in, minus what the channel cost, divided by what it cost.

Use [patient lifetime value](/blog/medspa-patient-lifetime-value/) rather than the first visit, or you'll cut channels that are actually profitable.

Ignore followers, impressions, and raw traffic unless they turn into booked patients.

The [med spa KPIs guide](/blog/medspa-kpis/) covers each metric in more depth.

## <span className="he">🚫</span> Why med spa marketing plans fail

Most failed plans don't fail for lack of effort.

They fail because the order was wrong, or because nobody could tell what was working.

<Compare>

<Side title="Plans that stall" tone="no">

- Ads before the site converts
- Every channel at once, none done well
- Judged by clicks, likes, and followers
- Deep discounts that attract one-time bargain hunters
- No owner and no monthly review

</Side>

<Side title="Plans that compound" tone="yes">

- Foundation first, then demand
- Two or three channels, done well
- Judged by cost per booked consult
- Offers that lead to a second visit
- One owner, same numbers, same day each month

</Side>

</Compare>

Compliance sinks plans too.

Health claims have to be truthful and never promise results, commercial email needs a working unsubscribe under CAN-SPAM, marketing texts need consent under the TCPA, and Meta and Google both restrict health and before-and-after ads.

The [med spa marketing laws guide](/blog/medspa-marketing-laws/) covers all of it in one place.
