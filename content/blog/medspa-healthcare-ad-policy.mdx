Med spa advertising lives in a restricted category, and owners who don't know that tend to learn it the hard way, through disapprovals and suspended accounts.

The good news is that compliant campaigns run just fine. You just have to build inside the rules instead of tripping over them.

## <span className="he">🔒</span> Why the rules exist

Both Google and Meta treat health and cosmetic procedures as sensitive, because they involve personal information people don't want used against them.

That drives most of the restrictions: limits on how you can target, what you can show, and what you can claim, all aimed at protecting user privacy around health.

Understanding the why makes the specific rules easier to follow, because they're consistent once you see the logic.

## <span className="he">🎯</span> Targeting limits

Personalized advertising for health-related audiences is restricted.

You generally can't target based on inferred health conditions or treatments someone appears interested in, which is the same principle behind [HIPAA-aware retargeting](/blog/medspa-retargeting/).

Build audiences on general engagement and location rather than sensitive attributes, and you stay on the right side of the line.

## <span className="he">🖼️</span> Creative and claims

<Callout type="warning">

Before-and-after imagery is heavily restricted, especially on Meta, and claims that read as guaranteed medical outcomes get disapproved.

Plan creative that doesn't depend on close-up before-and-afters, and keep language honest and outcome-neutral rather than promising results.

</Callout>

Rules shift over time, so check current policy, but the safe default is to assume before-and-afters may be limited and to lead with lifestyle, education, and offers instead.

## <span className="he">📉</span> Meta's health and wellness restrictions

In early 2025 Meta began limiting how advertisers it classifies as health and wellness, which includes many med spas, can use conversion data.

For those accounts, lower-funnel events such as a lead or a purchase on your own site may not be available for campaign optimization.

In practice that means a campaign often can't be told to optimize for "booked consult" the way an unrestricted account can.

Check Events Manager for a restriction notice on your pixel before you assume your campaigns are optimizing the way you set them up.

## <span className="he">✅</span> Compliant workarounds that still book consults

None of these get around the policy; they work inside it.

<Cards>

<Card icon="target" title="Optimize for what's allowed">

Optimize for what's still allowed, such as landing page views or on-platform [instant form leads](/blog/facebook-lead-forms-vs-landing-pages/), then judge results by your own booked-consult numbers.

</Card>

<Card icon="chart" title="Track outcomes yourself">

Tag every ad with UTMs and record lead source in your booking system or CRM, so you know which ads produce consults even when Meta can't see them.

</Card>

<Card icon="funnel" title="Let the page work harder">

When the algorithm can't optimize for the conversion, the page's own conversion rate matters more, which is what [CRO](/medspa-cro/) is for.

</Card>

</Cards>

## <span className="he">🧾</span> Running compliant campaigns

Getting approved is mostly about respecting the category from the start.

<Checklist>

- Keep targeting general, not health-attribute-based
- Use creative that doesn't rely on restricted imagery
- Avoid guaranteed-result claims in copy
- Send clicks to a clear, honest [landing page](/medspa-cro/) that matches the ad

</Checklist>
