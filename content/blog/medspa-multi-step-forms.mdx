- **Multi-step beats one long form** because momentum beats commitment shock.
- **Lead with the easiest question:** tappable cards, not a phone number.
- **The LaserAway pattern:** interest, then a qualifier, then contact last.
- **This site's audit form** is that exact pattern. Try it above.

A single long form asks a stranger for everything at once. A multi-step form asks one easy question, then builds momentum. For med spas, the multi-step version consistently wins, and it's usually the single highest-impact test on the site.

## <span className="he">🎯</span> Why single long forms bleed leads

Commitment psychology: the first click is the hardest.

<Compare>

<Side title="Single long form" tone="no">

- A wall of fields shown up front
- Triggers a quick bounce before anyone commits

</Side>

<Side title="Multi-step form" tone="yes">

- Leads with tappable cards, not a phone number
- Once someone answers a low-friction question, sunk-cost momentum carries them through the rest

</Side>

</Compare>

## <span className="he">🧩</span> The LaserAway form anatomy

The booking form our founder tested at [LaserAway](https://www.laseraway.com) broke the ask into steps that escalated gently:

<Steps>

<Step title="Treatment interest">

A low-friction, high-engagement opener (buttons, not a dropdown).

</Step>

<Step title="A qualifying question">

History or an age band.

</Step>

<Step title="Contact details">

Asked last, once the visitor is already invested.

</Step>

</Steps>

What got tested along the way: step count, icons versus text on the option cards, question ordering, auto-advance on selection, and the progress indicator.

Each variable was its own controlled test.

<Callout type="tip">

The audit form at the top of this page uses exactly this pattern: practice type first, contact last. Eat the dog food.

</Callout>

## <span className="he">⚠️</span> Fields that quietly kill conversion

<Cards>

<Card icon="x" title="Phone number first">

Asked before the visitor is invested.

</Card>

<Card icon="x" title="Free-text boxes">

Typing where a tap would do.

</Card>

<Card icon="x" title="Needless required fields">

Fields that aren't actually required to follow up.

</Card>

<Card icon="x" title="A message field">

Makes people feel they must compose something.

</Card>

</Cards>

## <span className="he">📊</span> Measuring per-step drop-off

Instrument each step as its own event so you can see exactly where people quit. The step with the biggest drop is your next test, not a guess.
