Laser hair removal marketing is less about finding people who want smooth skin and more about selling the full series to them at the right time of year.

Laser hair removal is one of the most searchable, most seasonal treatments a med spa offers, and one of the most commonly mis-sold.

The practices that win it understand two things: it's a package, and the best time to sell it is before patients think to buy.

This playbook comes from the inside: Gabe Meierotto, founder of More Booked Consults, spent 2018–2023 as Director of CRO at [LaserAway](https://www.laseraway.com), a chain built on laser hair removal, where he owned the testing roadmap across 100+ locations and grew sitewide conversion from 3 percent to 11 percent.

It covers the calendar, the offer, the ads, local search, the landing page, retention, and the numbers that tell you whether any of it is working.

If you run a dedicated laser practice, the [laser clinic marketing](/laser-clinic-marketing/) page covers the whole service mix.

The message should change with the calendar, not just the budget.

| Time of year | Who is buying | What to market |
|---|---|---|
| Fall and winter | Planners who want results by summer | The off-season start and the full package |
| Spring | Late starters with a deadline in mind | A realistic plan and a payment option |
| Summer | Existing patients and their friends | Added body areas and referral credits |

A single evergreen ad ignores all of that, which is why the creative should be refreshed every month.

The fastest way to break this is a deep-discount single session on a daily-deal site.

It fills a chair once, trains patients to wait for the next deal, and rarely turns into a package, which is the trade-off our [Groupon guide](/blog/medspa-groupon/) walks through.

Promotions can still work, as long as they add value to the series instead of cutting the price of one visit:

- **An added body area** when someone buys a full package
- **A payment plan** that turns a large package price into a manageable monthly number
- **An off-season start offer** that rewards booking in the fall
- **A referral credit** toward the next package, set up the way the [referral program guide](/blog/medspa-referral-programs/) describes
- **A maintenance membership** after the series ends, covered in the [membership programs guide](/blog/medspa-membership-programs/)

Price the package clearly on your page, because a stranger who can't find a number usually leaves to find one somewhere else.

### What Meta's rules mean for laser hair removal ads

Laser hair removal avoids the prescription-drug rules that complicate Botox ads, but it isn't free of restrictions.

Meta's [Health and Wellness ad standards](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness/) name laser treatments among the cosmetic procedures advertisers can promote, and they set the rules those ads have to follow.

- **Target 18 and older** for any cosmetic procedure ad.
- **No statements of inferiority,** so copy can't attack the viewer's body, skin, or hygiene.
- **No timeframe promises without qualifiers,** such as a guaranteed result by a set date.
- **Before-and-after images are allowed** under the current version, with written patient consent, as the [before-and-after photo guide](/blog/medspa-before-after-photos/) explains.

Meta can also restrict the data your pixel sends back if it classifies your site as health-related, which changes what its algorithm can optimize toward.

The [Meta health and wellness ad policy guide](/blog/medspa-healthcare-ad-policy/) explains both problems and the compliant workarounds.

Tracking pixels can also leak patient information, a HIPAA risk covered in the [website tracking guide](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/).

TikTok is another paid option some practices test, and our [TikTok ads guide](/blog/medspa-tiktok-ads/) covers when it's worth it.

### "Permanent hair reduction," not "permanent hair removal"

The claim in your ads should match the claim your device is cleared for.

FDA clearance documents for laser hair removal devices typically use the phrase "permanent hair reduction," defined as a long-term, stable reduction in the number of hairs regrowing when measured at 6, 9, and 12 months after a treatment regime, as in this [FDA 510(k) clearance for a diode laser](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf18/K180353.pdf).

That's a reduction in regrowth, not a promise that every hair is gone forever, so "permanent hair removal" overstates it.

Check your own device's clearance before you write the ad, and use the same wording on the landing page.

The [med spa marketing laws guide](/blog/medspa-marketing-laws/) covers the wider rules on claims.

## <span className="he">🧾</span> Landing pages that sell the laser hair removal series

Every channel above ends on a page, and that page decides whether the click becomes a booked consult.

A laser hair removal prospect has the same handful of questions every time, and a good page answers them in order.

<Checklist>

- Does it work on my skin and hair type?
- How many sessions will it take?
- What does a package cost, and can I pay over time?
- Does it hurt, and what should I do before the first visit?
- Can I see real results, with consent, in a before-and-after gallery?
- How do I book, in one short form?

</Checklist>

Send ad traffic to a dedicated page for one offer, not to your homepage, a point the [landing page guide](/blog/medspa-landing-pages/) breaks down section by section.

Conversion components help too, such as a multi-step form that asks the easy questions first or an exit-intent offer for the visitor about to leave.

## <span className="he">📱</span> Social media for laser hair removal clinics

Organic social media is where most laser hair removal clinics spend the most hours for the least return.

Our view is that organic posting is slow to produce results and may not work at all, so we tell clients to put that effort into paid ads instead.

If you still post, short-form video that shows the treatment room, answers common questions, and introduces your providers can help people who already follow you trust you.

Just don't count on social media to fill the schedule for a series that has to start in the fall.

## <span className="he">🔁</span> Keep patients through every laser hair removal session

A package only pays off if the patient finishes it, so retention is part of laser hair removal marketing.

- **Book the next visit before they leave,** at the interval your provider recommends.
- **Schedule the whole series up front** where your calendar allows it.
- **Send reminders** timed to that interval, not a generic monthly blast.
- **Follow up the same day** when someone misses a visit, before the gap turns into a dropout.
- **Offer the next body area** once the first series is going well.

The [client retention guide](/blog/medspa-client-retention/) goes deeper on rebooking.

The same speed matters at the start of the relationship: a new lead who waits hours for a reply often books with whoever answered first, which the [speed-to-lead guide](/blog/speed-to-lead-medspa/) explains.

Whatever CRM or booking software you use, set it up so every new request reaches a person who can call back quickly.

- **Cost per lead** is ad spend divided by form fills and calls, a starting point that the [cost per lead guide](/blog/medspa-cost-per-lead-benchmarks/) puts in context.

- **Return on ad spend** only means something when it's measured on packages sold, not on leads.
