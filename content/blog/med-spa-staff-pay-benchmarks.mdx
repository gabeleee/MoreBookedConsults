## <span className="he">💉</span> Injectors: part-time, contract and commission

Injector hiring looks different from every other role in the data.

Half of nurse injector listings (50%) are part-time, the highest share of any clinical role.

Aesthetic NPs are the most likely to be hired as contractors, at 38% of listings, against 24% for aesthetic RNs and 6% for nurse injectors.

The commission share matters when you read the pay figures.

Posted ranges are mostly base pay, and the variable part lives in the listing text, from "$45 to $50 per hour plus commission" to fully commission-based roles.

So an injector comparing your listing with the one next to it is weighing base, commission and schedule together, not one hourly number.

## <span className="he">⚖️</span> National chains vs independent med spas

We split employers into national chains (30 companies hiring in five or more cities) and independents (927 employers).

Neither group simply pays more: it depends on the role.

Chains posted higher medians for estheticians, NPs and managers, and lower ones for nurse injectors and patient coordinators.

The esthetician gap has the most data behind it, at $3.75 an hour, or about $7,800 a year on a full-time schedule.

The injector gap runs the other way and is larger, at $8.75 an hour, though only six chains posted injector pay.

Chains are also less likely to state pay on any given listing: 44% of chain listings do, against 58% of independent listings.

Per employer the two groups are close (67% of chains and 64% of independents post pay on at least one listing), so the gap comes from chains leaving pay off some locations.

## <span className="he">📞</span> The front desk books your consults

The two roles that answer most new patient calls and messages are also the lowest paid in the data.

Front desk coordinators post a median $20.75 an hour and patient coordinators $20.00.

47% of front desk listings are part-time, and only 16% of employers hiring for the role mention commission.

That's worth a second look if you spend money on ads or SEO.

Every inquiry those campaigns produce lands with this role, and how fast and how well it gets answered decides whether it turns into a booked consult.

Sales consultants, the role many chains use for that job, are paid the other way around: a low base ($22.00 an hour median) and commission at 57% of employers.

## <span className="he">🧭</span> What this means for practice owners

Check your posted pay against the middle half for the role, not the median alone.

A nurse injector listing at $45 an hour is at the bottom quarter of what candidates see, and one at $70 is at the top quarter.

If you pay commission, say so in the listing, with the structure.

Nearly a third of injector employers already do, and a base rate without it looks low next to theirs.

Post pay at all if you don't today.

Roughly a third of employers (36% of independents) never stated pay on any listing in the data, so a stated range already sets you apart from them.

And look at the front desk with the same eye you give your marketing.

It's the lowest paid role in the practice and the one that turns every paid inquiry into a booked consult or a lost one.

## <span className="he">🔬</span> How we did it

**Source.** Listings come from [AestheticHires](https://aesthetichires.com), which collects med spa and aesthetics openings from employer career sites, applicant tracking systems and job boards. We used every listing live on October 5, 2026: 2,683 listings from 957 employers in all 50 states and Washington, D.C..

**Roles.** Each listing is assigned a role from its title. Listings that didn't fit a defined role (232) are included in the overall counts but not in any role's figures.

**Pay.** We used the midpoint of each posted range. Each employer and range counts once per role, so a chain posting the same range at 50 locations counts once. Hourly and annual pay are kept separate, ranges outside $7.25–$300 an hour or $20,000–$500,000 a year are dropped as parsing errors, and a figure is shown only with at least 10 distinct ranges from at least 5 employers. Medians are rounded to the nearest $0.25 an hour or $500 a year.

**Chains.** A national chain is an employer hiring in five or more cities in the data (30 companies). Everyone else counts as independent.

**Listing text.** Commission, perks and training are counted per employer: an employer counts if any of its listings for that role mention it. Commission includes per-unit, production-based and retail commission.

**What it can't see.** Posted pay is what employers advertise, not what they end up paying, and it is mostly base pay. Listings without pay (50%) aren't in the pay figures, and employers that post pay may differ from those that don't.

**Data.** The figures by role and segment are available as a [CSV download](/data/med-spa-staff-pay-2026.csv). We don't publish individual listings or employer pay.
