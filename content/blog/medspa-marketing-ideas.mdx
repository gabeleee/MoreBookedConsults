## <span className="he">⚡</span> Start with the highest-return idea

Before any new campaign, fix the conversion rate on the traffic you already have.

It's not a "campaign," so it never makes these lists, but lifting your booking rate multiplies every other idea and costs no ad spend.

<Callout type="key">

A new promotion sends people to a leaky site, which wastes most of them.

Fixing [conversion](/medspa-cro/) first means every idea below works harder. Do this before you spend on anything new.

</Callout>

In practice that means a treatment page for each service, a clear price or price range, one obvious booking button, and a short [multi-step form](/blog/medspa-multi-step-forms/) instead of a long one.

An [exit-intent offer](/blog/medspa-exit-intent-popups/) can catch some of the visitors who were about to leave anyway.

## <span className="he">📍</span> Local SEO: get found by nearby clients

Patients often find a med spa by searching a treatment plus "near me" or their city, so ranking in Google's map pack and local results is one of the highest-value ideas on this list.

It's also one of our core services: [med spa SEO](/medspa-seo/) built on a topical map, so your site covers every treatment and question your city searches for.

The basics you can start on today:

- **Google Business Profile.** Choose the right primary category, list every treatment as a service, add real photos, and post updates. The [Google Business Profile guide](/blog/medspa-google-business-profile/) walks through every field.
- **Consistent name, address, and phone.** The same details on every [directory and citation](/blog/medspa-citations-directories/) help Google trust your listing.
- **A page per treatment.** Botox, fillers, microneedling, and laser each deserve their own page, targeting how patients actually search.
- **Reviews.** Recent, detailed reviews help both rankings and clicks (more on that below).

The [med spa SEO checklist](/blog/medspa-seo-checklist/) turns this into a to-do list.

Local SEO compounds: it's slower than ads, but it keeps working after you stop paying, and the topical authority it builds also tends to help you show up in AI search answers.

## <span className="he">📣</span> Med spa advertising ideas that book consults

If you want patients this month, paid ads are the fastest lever, and the platform choice matters more than most owners realize.

### Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram)

Meta is where we'd put a med spa's paid budget first.

Aesthetic treatments are visual, and Facebook and Instagram let you put them in front of nearby people before they start comparison shopping.

What works for us:

- **Fresh static image creative every month,** so the audience doesn't tune the ads out. See [med spa ad creative](/blog/medspa-ad-creative/) for what to test.
- **A dedicated landing page per offer,** not your homepage, and a test running on it.
- **[Retargeting](/blog/medspa-retargeting/)** people who visited a treatment page but didn't book.
- **Treatment-led offers,** like a Botox or [dermal filler](/blog/dermal-filler-marketing/) new-patient offer, instead of a generic "20% off everything."

The [Facebook and Instagram ads guide](/blog/medspa-facebook-instagram-ads/) covers setup, and our [managed med spa Facebook ads](/medspa-facebook-ads/) service is where we run it for you.

Meta has its own rules for health-related ads, so read the [healthcare ad policy guide](/blog/medspa-healthcare-ad-policy/) before you launch.

### Google Ads and pay-per-click

Google Ads (pay-per-click search ads) are on every list of med spa advertising ideas, and they can work.

Our view, from experience: clicks on aesthetic treatment searches are very expensive, and Meta ads usually get med spas better results for the money.

That's why we run Meta ads instead of Google Ads.

If you do try PPC, send each ad group to a matching treatment page, and judge it by cost per booked consult rather than clicks.

The [med spa Google Ads guide](/blog/medspa-google-ads/) covers the mechanics, and [SEO vs Google Ads](/blog/medspa-seo-vs-google-ads/) helps you decide where the budget belongs.

## <span className="he">📸</span> Content ideas: photos, blog posts, and social media

Content helps patients trust you before they book, but some formats pay back much faster than others.

**Before-and-after photos** are the most persuasive content a med spa has.

Get specific, written consent for each use, keep lighting and angles consistent, and read the [before-and-after photos guide](/blog/medspa-before-after-photos/) and [FTC testimonial rules](/blog/ftc-testimonial-rules-medspa/) before you post.

**Educational blog content** answers the questions patients search, like how long Botox lasts or what microneedling feels like.

Done around a topical map, it feeds your local SEO, and the [content marketing guide](/blog/medspa-content-marketing/) shows how to plan it.

**Organic social media** (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook) is on every list of ideas, so here's our honest take.

Posting is slow to produce results, and for many practices it never books many consults at all.

If you have limited time, we'd put that effort into paid Meta ads instead, and use your feed mainly as proof for people who find you elsewhere.

If you do post, a few formats are worth trying:

- **Short-form video** like Reels and TikToks showing a treatment or answering a question. See [video and YouTube](/blog/medspa-video-youtube/) and [TikTok organic](/blog/medspa-tiktok-organic/).
- **Live Q&A sessions** where an injector answers questions about a treatment.
- **User-generated content** and a branded hashtag, with patient permission.
- **Influencer partnerships** with local creators, covered in [influencer marketing](/blog/medspa-influencer-marketing/).

Expect these to build slowly.

If TikTok's audience fits your treatments, [TikTok ads](/blog/medspa-tiktok-ads/) are the faster route, and the [Instagram marketing guide](/blog/medspa-instagram-marketing/) covers the platform most med spas lean on.

## <span className="he">🔁</span> Email, SMS, and retention ideas that quietly out-earn the rest

The ideas owners overlook are the ones that keep patients coming back.

<Checklist>

- **[Membership programs](/blog/medspa-membership-programs/)** that turn one visit into recurring revenue.
- **Segmented [email campaigns](/blog/medspa-email-marketing/):** a welcome series, rebooking reminders timed to each treatment's cycle, and launches sent only to patients who'd care.
- **[SMS](/blog/medspa-sms-marketing/) reminders and win-backs** for upcoming appointments and lapsed patients.
- **A [rebooking habit](/blog/medspa-client-retention/)** so the next appointment is set before this one ends.

</Checklist>

A repeat patient costs far less than a new one, so retention ideas usually beat acquisition ideas on return, even though they get less attention.

Most of this runs from your booking software, and the [booking software guide](/blog/medspa-booking-software/) covers what to look for.

## <span className="he">📬</span> Print and direct mail

Print still has a place for a local business, if you keep it tight.

A postcard to nearby neighborhoods with one clear offer and a trackable code or landing page is easy to measure.

Brochures belong at your front desk and with partner businesses.

Billboards and broad mailers are hard to track, so test small before committing.

## <span className="he">📊</span> Measure every idea by booked consults

An idea only "works" if you can see it booking consults, so tracking comes before scaling any campaign.

<Steps>

<Step title="Tag every source">

Give each campaign, partner, and mailer its own link or code, so you know where each lead came from.

</Step>

<Step title="Track conversions, not clicks">

Count form fills and calls, then tie them to booked consults. The [attribution and call tracking guide](/blog/medspa-attribution-call-tracking/) shows how.

</Step>

<Step title="Keep patient data out of ad pixels">

</Step>

<Step title="Review monthly">

Compare cost per booked consult across channels using the [med spa KPIs](/blog/medspa-kpis/), then cut what isn't paying.

</Step>

</Steps>
