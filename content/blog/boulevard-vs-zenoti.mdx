- **Boulevard** suits single and small multi-location med spas that sell on client experience.
- **Zenoti** suits chains that need enterprise controls and deep clinical charting across many locations.
- **Pricing:** Boulevard publishes it ($143 to $410/month per location in September 2026); Zenoti is quote only.
- **Setup:** Boulevard is typically live faster; Zenoti usually takes several weeks.
- **Too big a bill for either?** Mangomint is the usual alternative for smaller med spas.

Boulevard and Zenoti end up on the same shortlist because both can run a med spa end to end: scheduling, payments, memberships, forms, charting, and marketing.

They get there from opposite directions.

Boulevard started as a client-experience platform for salons and spas and has added clinical depth for med spas.

Zenoti started as an enterprise system for large wellness chains and brings that weight to every account, big or small.

Which one fits depends mostly on how many locations you run and how clinical your treatment menu is.

This comparison covers both, then adds the lens most software reviews skip: what each one does to the patient trying to book.

Most med spas need clinical forms and charting, which is why the realistic Boulevard number is the Forms add-on or Prestige, not the entry plan.

ePrescribe adds $45 per prescriber per month if you need it.

Zenoti doesn't publish prices at all.

Multi-location pricing is custom by design, and single-location med spas commonly report quotes in the $300 to $600 per month range depending on the modules they pick.

Its AI add-ons (AI Plus for marketing and operations, AI Workforce for calls, web chat, and texts) are priced separately on top of the core system.

Get a written quote for your exact setup (locations, providers, charting, texting volume) before you compare, because the entry plan is rarely what a med spa actually pays.

## <span className="he">🩺</span> Clinical charting and compliance

This is where the two differ most for an injector-led practice.

Zenoti's charting is its strength: consent forms, medical history, injection mapping, and product-specific unit tracking are core to how it runs clinical practices.

Boulevard has closed much of the gap with its Forms product, which covers treatment-specific templates, before-and-after photos with markup, injection mapping, and secure storage built for HIPAA.

The difference is mostly depth and reporting across locations, not whether the basics exist.

If you run one or two locations with a standard injectables and laser menu, Boulevard's charting will likely cover you.

If you run many locations with medical directors who need consistent clinical records and unit reporting across all of them, Zenoti's depth starts paying for itself.

Whatever you pick, the HIPAA question doesn't stop at the software, so read our guide to [HIPAA and med spa marketing](/blog/hipaa-medspa-marketing/) before you wire tracking into your booking pages.

## <span className="he">🏢</span> Multi-location and reporting

Zenoti was built for chains.

Shared patient records, centralized campaigns, and location-level KPIs are standard, and it's the reason large groups choose it.

Boulevard supports multiple locations, but it's priced and managed location by location, with the more advanced multi-location reporting on its higher tiers and Enterprise plan.

## <span className="he">📱</span> The booking flow: the conversion lens

Most reviews stop at back-office features.

But your booking widget is embedded on your website and linked from your ads, which makes it one of the most important conversion elements you have.

A slow load, an account wall before times appear, or availability buried three screens deep loses bookings your marketing already paid for.

Boulevard's reputation is built on this: its client-facing booking and intake flow is widely rated as one of the most polished in the category.

Zenoti's flow is capable and highly configurable, and it also offers branded apps for larger groups, but it takes more setup to make it feel effortless.

Don't take anyone's word for it, including ours.

Run this test on each platform's live booking flow, on your own phone, before you sign:

<Checklist>

- Time from tapping "Book" to seeing real available times
- Whether an account or password is required before booking
- Number of screens to finish a single Botox appointment
- Whether new patients can book a consult without calling
- How the deposit or card-on-file step is explained
- Whether the confirmation text arrives within a minute

</Checklist>

The platform that feels effortless to you will feel effortless to a patient comparing three med spas on a lunch break.

If the native widget still feels clunky, a [multi-step form on your own site](/blog/medspa-multi-step-forms/) that hands off to the booking system can convert better than the widget alone.

## <span className="he">🛠️</span> Setup and switching

Boulevard assigns an onboarding specialist at sign-up, and most med spas are live within a few weeks.

Zenoti typically takes four to six weeks for setup and data migration, with a dedicated implementation manager, which is reasonable for a chain and heavy for a single location.

The hidden cost of switching is marketing, not software.

Every booking link in your Google Business Profile, Instagram bio, ads, emails, and website changes the day you move.

List every place your old booking URL appears before you switch, update them on launch day, and click each one yourself.

Membership and package balances deserve the same care, since [members](/blog/medspa-membership-programs/) who find their credits missing are the patients you can least afford to annoy.

## <span className="he">🔀</span> When neither: Mangomint and other alternatives

The most common search after "Boulevard vs Zenoti" is "what's the best alternative to Boulevard."

For a smaller, injectable-led med spa, the usual answer is Mangomint.

It runs one plan at $120 per month per location plus $10 per user, with forms, charting, memberships, and online booking included and no contract.

Its interface is among the best rated in the category, and for a practice with one location and a handful of providers it often costs less than Boulevard with forms added.

Mangomint vs Zenoti is rarely a close call: Mangomint for small and simple, Zenoti for large and complex.

Vagaro is the budget option, cheaper than all three, with a less premium patient experience.

## <span className="he">✅</span> How to decide

Choose Boulevard if you run one to a few locations, sell on client experience, and want published pricing and a fast setup.

Choose Zenoti if you run many locations, need consistent clinical records across them, and have the staff to configure an enterprise system.

Choose Mangomint if you're a smaller med spa and Boulevard's price with forms feels steep.

Then run the booking-flow test on your finalists, because the software your patients touch matters more to your revenue than the software your staff does.
