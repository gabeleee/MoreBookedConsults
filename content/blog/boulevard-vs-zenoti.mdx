Zenoti vs Boulevard is the comparison most growing med spas end up making, because both platforms can run the whole practice: scheduling, payments, memberships, forms, charting, and marketing.

They get there from opposite directions.

Boulevard started as a client-experience platform for salons and spas and has added clinical depth for med spas.

Zenoti started as an enterprise system for large wellness chains and brings that weight to every account, big or small.

Which one fits depends mostly on how many locations you run and how clinical your treatment menu is.

This comparison covers pricing, features, AI tools, and setup, then adds the lens most software reviews skip: what each one does to the patient trying to book.

## <span className="he">🩺</span> Clinical charting and compliance

This is where the two differ most for an injector-led practice.

Zenoti's charting is its strength: digital intake and consent forms, medical history, injection records, and e-prescribing are core to how it runs clinical practices.

Its injectable records capture the product, units per zone, lot number, and expiry date, and inventory updates as the chart is completed.

Boulevard has closed much of the gap with its Forms product, which covers intake forms, treatment-specific templates, treatment photos with markup, and injection mapping.

Boulevard tracks inventory as products are used, but its public pages don't describe lot-number tracking, so ask to see it in the demo if your medical director needs it.

The difference is mostly depth and reporting across locations, not whether the basics exist.

If you run one or two locations with a standard injectables and laser menu, Boulevard's charting will likely cover you.

If you run many locations with medical directors who need consistent clinical records and unit reporting across all of them, Zenoti's depth starts paying for itself.

### HIPAA and the signed BAA

Any vendor that stores patient health information for you should sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA).

Zenoti's med spa features page says it provides a signed BAA.

Boulevard's own HIPAA guide tells buyers that any vendor handling patient data must sign one before you use the platform.

Don't rely on either page: ask each vendor for the signed BAA before you migrate a single chart.

The HIPAA question doesn't stop at the software, either, so read how [tracking pixels on booking pages can leak patient data](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/) before you add ad tracking to your booking flow.

## <span className="he">🤖</span> AI features: Zeenie, AI Scribe, and AI receptionists

Both vendors are adding AI quickly, and this is the row most likely to be out of date by the time you read it.

Zenoti has the broader set today.

Zeenie is its built-in assistant: staff ask it questions about the business in plain language and it pulls reports, guides tasks, and drafts marketing messages.

AI Scribe listens to the provider and patient during the visit and drafts structured, editable chart notes, with aesthetics terminology such as injectable product names and treatment zones.

Its wider AI Workforce adds agents such as an AI Receptionist and an AI Lead Manager, and those need the AI Plus package.

Boulevard's live AI product is Beau, an AI receptionist that answers calls, helps clients book, and captures leads around the clock.

Boulevard's own blog (September 2026) describes its AI Scribe as in development, so treat it as a roadmap item until it ships.

If AI charting is a deciding factor for your injectors, Zenoti has it now and Boulevard doesn't yet.

## <span className="he">🏢</span> Multi-location, payroll, and reporting

Zenoti was built for chains.

Shared patient records, membership balances visible across locations, and location-level reporting are standard, and it's the reason large groups choose it.

Zenoti Integrated Payroll calculates pay from services, commissions, tips, and retail sales, which matters once you have dozens of providers on different pay plans.

Boulevard supports multiple locations, but it's priced and managed location by location, with the more advanced multi-location reporting on its higher tiers and Enterprise plan.

Boulevard handles payroll through commission tracking and reports you export to the payroll processor you already use.

## <span className="he">📱</span> The booking flow: the conversion lens

Most reviews stop at back-office features.

But your booking widget is embedded on your website and linked from your ads, which makes it one of the most important conversion elements you have.

A slow load, an account wall before times appear, or availability buried three screens deep loses bookings your marketing already paid for.

Boulevard's reputation is built on this: its client-facing booking and intake flow is its main selling point.

Zenoti's flow is capable and highly configurable, and it also offers branded apps for larger groups, but it takes more setup to make it feel effortless.

Some platforms, such as Vagaro and Mindbody, also list you in a consumer marketplace where patients browse many businesses at once.

That exposure can help, but a patient who books through a marketplace is comparing you with the practice next to you, while a patient who books on your own site stays on your page, which is why we care most about [the booking path on your website](/medspa-cro/).

Don't take anyone's word for the booking flow, including ours.

Run this test on each platform's live booking flow, on your own phone, before you sign:

<Checklist>

- Time from tapping "Book" to seeing real available times
- Whether an account or password is required before booking
- Number of screens to finish a single Botox appointment
- Whether new patients can book a consult without calling
- How the deposit or card-on-file step is explained
- Whether the confirmation text arrives within a minute

</Checklist>

The platform that feels effortless to you will feel effortless to a patient comparing three med spas on a lunch break.

If the native widget still feels clunky, a [multi-step form on your own site](/blog/medspa-multi-step-forms/) that hands off to the booking system can convert better than the widget alone.

## <span className="he">🛠️</span> Setup and switching

Boulevard assigns a dedicated Onboarding Specialist at sign-up to handle setup, data migration, and staff training.

Zenoti's own review page says its implementation typically takes four to 12 weeks, depending on locations, data migration, and how fast your team completes setup and training.

That's reasonable for a chain and heavy for a single location.

### Can you take your data with you?

Both vendors migrate data for new customers, and both say what moves depends on the system you're leaving.

The harder question is leaving them later, so ask before you sign:

<Checklist>

- Which client records, charts, and photos you can export
- Whether membership, package, and gift card balances export
- What file format the export comes in
- Whether there's a fee or a notice period to get it

</Checklist>

The hidden cost of switching is marketing, not software.

Every booking link in your Google Business Profile, Instagram bio, ads, emails, and website changes the day you move.

List every place your old booking URL appears before you switch, update them on launch day, and click each one yourself.

Membership and package balances deserve the same care, since [members](/blog/medspa-membership-programs/) who find their credits missing are the patients you can least afford to annoy.

## <span className="he">🔀</span> When neither fits: Mangomint and other alternatives

The most common search after "Zenoti vs Boulevard" is "what's the best alternative to Boulevard."

Owners looking at Zenoti alternatives usually want the opposite of what Zenoti is: published pricing, no long commitment, and a setup measured in days rather than weeks.

For a smaller, injectable-led med spa, the usual answer is Mangomint.

As of September 2026, it runs one plan at $120 per month per location plus $10 per user, with forms and charting, memberships and packages, online booking, and payments included, and no contracts.

Payroll is an optional add-on there, and Mangomint advertises free onboarding and data transfer.

Mangomint vs Boulevard comes down to price and tiers: for one location and a handful of providers, Mangomint usually costs less than Boulevard with Forms added, while Boulevard offers an Enterprise plan and a built-in AI receptionist.

Mangomint vs Zenoti is rarely a close call: Mangomint for small and simple, Zenoti for large and complex.

Vagaro is the budget-focused option, with a less premium patient experience.

Mindbody, Jane App, and Pabau also come up, with Mindbody known for its consumer app and Jane App and Pabau built around clinic charting.

## <span className="he">✅</span> How to decide

Choose Boulevard if you run one to a few locations, sell on client experience, and want published pricing and a guided setup.

Choose Zenoti if you run many locations, need consistent clinical records, lot tracking, and payroll across them, and have the staff to configure an enterprise system.

Choose Mangomint if you're a smaller med spa and Boulevard's price with Forms feels steep.

Then run the booking-flow test on your finalists, because the software your patients touch matters more to your revenue than the software your staff does.
