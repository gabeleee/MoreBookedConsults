## <span className="he">👥</span> Know your patients and your competition

A breast augmentation patient and a facelift patient are different people, with different ages, worries, research habits and budgets.

Build a short patient persona for each of your top procedures: who they are, what they fear, what proof they need, and what makes them book.

Those personas decide your ad targeting and the message on each landing page, so one generic "cosmetic surgery" pitch never has to speak to everyone.

Then study the local competition without copying it.

<Checklist>

- Search your top five procedures plus your city, and note who shows up in the map pack and the first organic results.
- Open their procedure pages and compare: pricing signals, financing, gallery depth, review counts, and how easy the consult form is.
- Note the questions they answer that you don't.
- Look at which of them run Facebook and Instagram ads, using Meta's public Ad Library.

</Checklist>

The gaps you find become your content plan and your test list.

## <span className="he">⭐</span> Reviews, testimonials and before-and-after ethics

For a surgical decision, patients read reviews closely, and Google weighs them in local rankings.

Ask every satisfied patient the same way, at the same point in their journey, so your review profile reflects your real patients rather than a campaign.

The FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials (16 CFR Part 465, in effect since October 2024) bans fake reviews, buying reviews conditioned on a positive sentiment, and suppressing negative ones.

When you reply to a negative review, stay calm and brief, and never confirm that the reviewer is a patient or discuss their care, because that can be a HIPAA violation.

Patient testimonials on your own site follow the FTC's Endorsement Guides: they must reflect honest experiences, and they shouldn't imply that one patient's result is typical if it isn't.

Before-and-after photos need written consent that covers marketing use, consistent lighting and angles, and no retouching of the result.

The detail is in [reviews and RealSelf for plastic surgeons](/blog/plastic-surgery-reviews-realself/), the [FTC testimonial rules](/blog/ftc-testimonial-rules-medspa/), and [before-and-after galleries](/blog/plastic-surgery-before-after-galleries/).

## <span className="he">🛡️</span> Patient privacy: tracking pixels and HIPAA

<Callout type="warning">

Ad pixels and analytics on procedure pages or booking confirmations can send data that ties a person to a treatment to a third-party ad platform.

HHS's Office for Civil Rights issued guidance on online tracking technologies in December 2022 (updated in March 2024), and a federal court vacated part of it in June 2024 as applied to public, unauthenticated pages.

The safe posture is still to collect less and configure tracking so it never passes health context to ad platforms.

</Callout>

This is guidance, not legal advice; [HIPAA and website tracking](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/) explains where the exposure hides and how to measure marketing without it.
