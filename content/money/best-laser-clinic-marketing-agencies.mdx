A laser clinic isn't marketed like a general med spa.

It sells packages, not single visits, because laser hair removal and most laser treatments need a series of sessions.

## <span className="he">🧭</span> What good laser clinic marketing looks like

Whichever agency you pick, these are the things to check in the plan they send you.

### It sells the series, not the session

A laser clinic's marketing should sell the package: the body area, the number of sessions, and the package price or monthly payment, stated plainly.

A deep-discount single session fills a chair once and rarely turns into a package, a trade-off the [Groupon guide](/blog/medspa-groupon/) walks through.

The [laser hair removal marketing](/blog/laser-hair-removal-marketing/) playbook covers package framing and value-adding offers in depth.

### It markets the off-season start

Laser hair removal takes a series of sessions and works best away from sun exposure, so the patients who want results by summer need to start in fall or winter.

Ask any agency when it plans to start the work, and what the message will be in each season.

### It wins "laser hair removal near me" treatment by treatment

Local laser searches are answered by the map pack first, so the plan should cover your Google Business Profile, consistent name, address and phone number across directories, and a page for each treatment you sell.

The [laser clinic SEO](/laser-clinic-seo/) page explains why a clinic can rank for hair removal and still be invisible for tattoo removal in the same city.

### Its claims match your device's clearance

FDA clearance documents for laser hair removal devices typically use "permanent hair reduction," a long-term, stable reduction in regrowth, as in this [FDA 510(k) clearance for a diode laser](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf18/K180353.pdf).

"Permanent hair removal" overstates that, so ask how the agency writes laser claims, and see the [med spa marketing laws guide](/blog/medspa-marketing-laws/) for the wider rules.

Meta also has its own [health and wellness ad rules](/blog/medspa-healthcare-ad-policy/) for cosmetic procedures.

### Its landing pages are built to book

Ad traffic should land on a page for one offer that answers the laser buyer's questions (skin type, sessions, package price, pain, booking) in order.

The [landing page guide](/blog/medspa-landing-pages/) breaks that page down section by section.

### Its tracking is HIPAA-aware and its reports count packages

Tracking pixels on lead forms can send patient health information to ad platforms, so read [HIPAA and website tracking](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/) before any agency installs tracking on your site.

The monthly report should tie leads to booked consults and packages sold, not just clicks and form fills.
