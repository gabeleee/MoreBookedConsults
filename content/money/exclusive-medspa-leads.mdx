- **Exclusive means one recipient: you.** Nobody else gets that person's name and number.
- **Shared leads are the industry's open wound,** the same prospect sold to three or four competing spas at once.
- **The difference is close rate, not sticker price.** A cheap lead that four spas chase is the expensive one.

If you have ever bought med spa leads and watched them go nowhere, this page explains why, and it is probably not the reason you were told.

The problem usually was not your front desk, your follow-up, or your offer.

The problem was that you were sold a shared lead.

Understanding that one distinction is the difference between believing "paid leads don't work" and running a lead channel that actually books patients.

## <span className="he">🔒</span> What "exclusive" actually means

An exclusive lead goes to exactly one practice.

You.

A shared lead is the opposite.

<Compare>

<Side title="Exclusive lead" tone="yes">

- Nobody else receives that person's name and number, so nobody else is calling them
- The prospect experiences a single practice reaching out, not a pile-on

</Side>

<Side title="Shared lead" tone="no">

- The same form fill is sold to three or four competing spas at once, sometimes more
- The broker makes more money per lead, because they sell the same prospect several times
- You make far less, because you are now one of four practices racing to the same phone

</Side>

</Compare>

## <span className="he">🪣</span> Why shared leads convert so badly

Picture the prospect on the other end.

They filled out one form, curious about Botox, and within an hour four different med spas are calling and texting them.

That does not feel like service.

It feels like they did something wrong, and the instinct is to stop answering entirely.

<Callout type="key">

Shared leads don't fail because the prospect wasn't interested. They fail because being sold to four competitors at once turns a warm inquiry into spam.

Even a perfect front desk can only win a fraction of a lead three other spas are also working, so most of your effort is spent on bookings someone else closes.

</Callout>

This is the whole reason "bought med spa leads don't convert" became conventional wisdom.

It is true, for shared leads.

It is not true for leads that only you receive, and conflating the two is what keeps good practices out of a channel that could work for them.

## <span className="he">💵</span> The price comparison people get wrong

Shared leads look cheaper on the sticker, and that is exactly the trap.

A $20 shared lead sold to four spas is not a $20 lead to you.

It is a $20 lead with a roughly one-in-four ceiling on your ability to close it, before anything else goes wrong.

A lead nobody else has has no such ceiling: if your team calls it fast and handles it well, the only thing standing between you and the booking is the prospect.

The right way to compare any lead source is close rate against [patient value](/blog/botox-cost-per-lead/), not sticker against sticker.

On that comparison, a lead you are not sharing with three competitors wins almost every time.

## <span className="he">🧐</span> How to tell whether a vendor's leads are exclusive

"Exclusive" is the easiest word in lead generation to print and the hardest to verify.

If you do buy from a vendor, ask a few direct questions before you sign.

<Cards>

<Card icon="search" title="Where does the traffic come from?">

A vendor who can't show you the ads and the landing page is asking you to trust a black box.

</Card>

<Card icon="shield" title="Is it sold to anyone else?">

Is the lead sold to anyone else, in any market, at any point? Get the answer in writing, including what happens to leads you don't respond to.

</Card>

<Card icon="link" title="What happens to the data if you leave?">

If the vendor owns the ad account, the pixel, and the audience, you leave with nothing.

</Card>

<Card icon="page" title="Can you see the raw form fills?">

Timestamps and source data are how you spot recycled or aged leads.

</Card>

</Cards>

Most vendors pass some of these questions.

Very few pass all of them, because the broker model depends on keeping the machine out of view.
