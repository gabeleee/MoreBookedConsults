## <span className="he">✅</span> Plastix Marketing review: what does it do well?

<Photo src="/images/agencies/plastix.webp" small="/images/agencies/plastix-800.webp" alt="Plastix Marketing medical spa marketing page" width="1280" height="700" caption="plastixmarketing.com, September 2026" />

[Plastix Marketing](https://plastixmarketing.com/medical-spa-marketing/) is headquartered in Greenville, South Carolina, and describes itself as a full-service growth marketing agency working exclusively with aesthetic practices: plastic surgeons, med spas, dermatologists, cosmetic dentists and orthodontists.

A fair summary of its strengths, from its own site:

- **One connected system:** SEO, AI search optimization (AEO and GEO), content, social media, Google, Meta, native and geofencing ads, web design, branding, video, chatbots, marketing automation, sales enablement and hosting, all run by one team.
- **CRM included:** it's a certified partner with HubSpot, Keap and SharpSpring, and sets up, integrates and supports your CRM as part of every Growth Plan, and says it can connect it to your website and even your electronic health records.
- **A BAA in writing:** its terms say it signs a Business Associate Agreement before accessing any practice system that holds patient health information, and it sells HIPAA-compliant hosting.
- **Published contract terms:** its Master Terms of Service spell out the initial term, renewal, notice period and what happens to your ad accounts and work if you leave.
- **Proof (self-reported):** Buckhead Plastic Surgery, a 164% lead increase and 3,500+ new leads in 18 months; it also says its average client stays 3.2 years.

### More Booked Consults: best if you already have a CRM and site and need more booked consults

**Best for:** med spas and aesthetic practices that already get traffic and inquiries but don't book enough of them.

More Booked Consults is a med spa and plastic surgery marketing agency focused on conversion (landing pages, CRO), local SEO and managed Meta ads.

It's run by Gabe Meierotto, LaserAway's in-house Director of CRO from 2018 to 2023, and works only with aesthetic practices.

- **Services:** conversion-focused landing pages, [conversion rate optimization](/medspa-cro/) including new components like multi-step forms and exit-intent modals, [local SEO](/medspa-seo/) built on topical maps with Google Business Profile work and citation cleanup, and [managed Meta ads](/medspa-advertising/) with fresh static image creative every month.
- **Pricing (published):** CRO $2,500/month, local SEO $2,500/month, both for $5,000/month; managed Meta ads $3,000/month plus a $2,500 one-time setup, with ad spend (minimum $3,000/month) paid by you directly to Meta and never marked up.

At LaserAway, Gabe's testing program took sitewide conversion from 3% to 11% (see [results](/results/)); those are his results in a former in-house role, not agency client results.

**Choose it over Plastix Marketing if** you already run a CRM and a website you're keeping, and your gap is a site that doesn't turn visitors into booked consults, weak map-pack visibility, or [Meta ads](/medspa-facebook-ads/) that need landing pages built to convert.

## <span className="he">📝</span> Questions to ask any agency before you switch

Get these answered in writing before you sign anything new, whichever agency you pick.

<Checklist>

- What are the exit terms: initial term, renewal, notice period, and any early-termination fee?
- Who owns the ad accounts, the website, the domain and the Google Business Profile, and are they in my name today?
- Will you sign a BAA, and how does your tracking avoid sending patient health data to ad platforms?
- Will your monthly report show booked consults by channel, not just leads and clicks?
- What happens to my landing pages, forms and automations if I leave: do I keep them, and where are they hosted?
- Can I export my contacts, lead history and campaign data before I give notice?

</Checklist>

Ask the outgoing agency the same ownership questions before you give notice.

Tracking pixels on lead forms can leak patient information, so read [HIPAA and website tracking](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/) before any new agency installs tracking on your site.
