## <span className="he">✅</span> What good med spa SEO looks like

Whichever company you hire, these six things separate real local SEO from monthly reports.

### A page for every treatment, and every treatment in every city you serve

Patients search "lip filler" or "laser hair removal" plus their city, not "med spa."

Good SEO starts with [keyword research](/blog/medspa-keyword-research/) that turns those searches into a topical map: one page per treatment, linked so Google sees you cover the whole subject.

### A complete Google Business Profile

Most of a med spa's local visibility starts in the map pack.

Check that the company sets the right primary category, lists every treatment as a service and keeps one profile per location; the [Google Business Profile guide](/blog/medspa-google-business-profile/) covers each setting.

### Consistent citations

An old address or phone number left on directories splits your signal across the web.

Ask how the company finds and fixes those listings; [citations and directories](/blog/medspa-citations-directories/) explains what matters.

### Technical basics before advanced tactics

A slow, hard-to-crawl site holds back every page on it.

The [med spa SEO checklist](/blog/medspa-seo-checklist/) lists the fundamentals any company should cover first.

### Reporting that reaches consults

Rankings and traffic are inputs, not the outcome.

A good report shows map-pack position, Google Business Profile calls and clicks, and consult requests by page; [attribution and call tracking](/blog/medspa-attribution-call-tracking/) shows how to connect them.

If rankings climb and consults don't, the gap is conversion, which is what [med spa CRO](/medspa-cro/) fixes.

### Tracking that doesn't leak patient data

Tracking pixels on forms and booking pages can send patient health information to ad and analytics platforms.

Read [HIPAA and website tracking](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/) before any company installs tracking on your site.

AI search visibility is worth asking about too.

In our experience it mostly follows from strong topical SEO, so judge it as part of the SEO work rather than a separate line item.

<Checklist>

- Will you build a page for each treatment, and each treatment-and-city combination we serve?
- How do you handle our Google Business Profile and citations, and how often?
- Do you guarantee rankings? (The honest answer is no.)
- Will the monthly report show consult requests, not just rankings and traffic?
- What is the price, the minimum term and the notice period?
- Do we own the pages, content, Google Business Profile and data if we leave?
- How does your tracking avoid sending patient health data to third parties?

</Checklist>
