## <span className="he">✅</span> What good cosmetic dermatology marketing looks like

Whichever agency you hire, check for these six things.

### A cosmetic menu that stands apart from medical care

A cosmetic patient shouldn't have to click past insurance pages and condition lists to find Botox or laser.

Each cosmetic treatment needs its own page with results, downtime, price guidance and one clear way to book, as the [dermatology marketing](/dermatology-marketing/) guide lays out.

### Provider credentials on every treatment page

Physician training is a dermatology practice's edge over the med spa down the street.

Name the dermatologist who performs or supervises each treatment, link to their provider page, and show their credentials where patients decide.

### Local SEO for each treatment and location

Cosmetic searches are local, so the practice needs treatment-and-city pages, a complete [Google Business Profile](/blog/medspa-google-business-profile/) and [consistent citations](/blog/medspa-citations-directories/).

A practice with several locations needs this for every location.

### Booking that works with several providers

Cosmetic demand is lost when a consult gets pushed weeks out behind medical visits.

Ask how the agency will route cosmetic inquiries to the right provider and put a booking link on each treatment page; the [booking software guide](/blog/medspa-booking-software/) covers what to look for.

### Tracking that doesn't leak patient data

Tracking pixels on forms and booking pages can send data that ties a person to a treatment to ad platforms.

Read [HIPAA and website tracking](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/) and ask who signs a BAA before any agency installs tracking.

### Reporting on booked consults by treatment

Rankings, clicks and leads are inputs.

The report should show consult requests and booked consults for each cosmetic treatment; [attribution and call tracking](/blog/medspa-attribution-call-tracking/) explains how to connect them.
