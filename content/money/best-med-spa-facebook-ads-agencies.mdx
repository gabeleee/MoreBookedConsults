## <span className="he">🎯</span> What good Facebook ads management looks like for a med spa

Whichever agency you shortlist, check these six things before you sign.

### Does it know Meta's rules for aesthetics ads?

Ads for cosmetic procedures must target people 18 and older, and before-and-after images are allowed only within Meta's current health and wellness standard.

Botox and other neuromodulators are prescription drugs, which brings extra restrictions on how the ad can name them.

Our guide to [Meta's health and wellness ad restrictions](/blog/medspa-healthcare-ad-policy/) covers the rules and the compliant workarounds.

### Where does the click land?

The page behind the ad decides whether a click becomes a booked consult.

<Compare>

<Side title="Meta instant lead form" tone="neutral">

- Never leaves the app, so more leads at a lower cost per lead
- Lower intent, so fewer of them book
- No page for you to test

</Side>

<Side title="Dedicated landing page" tone="yes">

- One offer, one path, one form
- Fewer leads, usually more likely to book
- A page you can A/B test every month

</Side>

</Compare>

The full trade-off is in [Facebook lead forms vs landing pages](/blog/facebook-lead-forms-vs-landing-pages/).

### How often is the creative refreshed?

The same ads shown to the same local audience for months tend to stop working.

Ask how many new ads you'll get and how often, then check that against the contract; our guide to [med spa ad creative](/blog/medspa-ad-creative/) covers what to test.

### Who holds the ad account and the ad spend?

The ad account should be in your name, and ad spend should be billed by Meta to you.

Ask whether the fee is flat or a percentage of spend, and whether any spend is marked up.

### What does the report show?

Clicks and cost per lead are inputs; the outcome is booked consults.

Ask for a sample report, and see [attribution and call tracking](/blog/medspa-attribution-call-tracking/) for how to connect leads to bookings.

### Is the tracking safe for patient data?

Meta pixels on lead forms can send health information to Meta.

Read [HIPAA and website tracking](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/) before any agency installs tracking on your site.

<Checklist>

- Where exactly will the ads send people, and do you test that page?
- How many new ads will I get each month?
- Is the ad account in my name, and does Meta bill me directly?
- Is your fee flat, and do you mark up or take a percentage of ad spend?
- What is the minimum term, the notice period, and any early-termination fee?
- Can I see a sample report that ties leads to booked consults?
- How does your tracking avoid sending patient health data to Meta?

</Checklist>
