## <span className="he">✅</span> What good plastic surgery SEO looks like

Whichever company you hire, these six things separate real surgical SEO from monthly reports.

### A page for every procedure you want more of

Patients search "rhinoplasty," "tummy tuck" or "breast augmentation surgeon" plus their city, not "plastic surgeon."

Good SEO starts with [keyword research](/blog/plastic-surgery-keyword-research/) that turns those searches into a topical map, then builds one thorough page per procedure covering candidacy, recovery, results, risks and cost.

The [procedure pages guide](/blog/plastic-surgery-procedure-pages-seo/) shows what each page needs to rank and convert.

### Surgeon and practice authority

Google treats surgical content as "Your Money or Your Life" content, held to a higher standard.

Check that the company puts a named surgeon on the content as author or reviewer, with accurate credentials, and keeps each surgeon's name consistent across the website, Google and directories.

### A deliberate plan for Google Business Profiles

Multi-surgeon practices have to decide how the practice listing and each surgeon's listing fit together, or they split their ranking signal.

Ask how the company handles that, and read [Google Business Profile for surgeons](/blog/plastic-surgery-google-business-profile/) before the call.

If you have more than one office, each location needs its own profile and its own local page; see [multi-location SEO for plastic surgery](/blog/plastic-surgery-multi-location-seo/).

### Before-and-after galleries Google can read

A gallery organized by procedure, linked to each procedure page, with descriptive file names, alt text and captions, gives Google more to rank and patients a reason to inquire.

Only publish photos with the patient's written authorization for that use, and never put a patient's name in a file name or caption; the [before-and-after gallery guide](/blog/plastic-surgery-before-after-galleries/) covers layout and consent.

### Reporting that reaches consults

Rankings and traffic are inputs, not the outcome.

A good report shows map-pack position for your core procedures, Google Business Profile calls and clicks, and consult requests by page; [attribution and call tracking](/blog/medspa-attribution-call-tracking/) shows how to connect them.

If rankings climb and consults don't, the gap is conversion, which is what [plastic surgery CRO](/plastic-surgery-cro/) fixes.

### Tracking that doesn't leak patient data

Tracking pixels on procedure pages and consult forms can send data tying a person to a procedure to ad and analytics platforms.

Read [HIPAA and website tracking](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/) before any company installs tracking on your site.

AI search visibility is worth asking about too.

In our experience it mostly follows from strong topical SEO, so judge it as part of the SEO work rather than a separate line item.

<Checklist>

- Will you build a page for each procedure, and each procedure-and-city combination we serve?
- How will you handle our practice listing versus each surgeon's listing?
- How do you make our before-and-after gallery searchable without exposing patient details?
- Is a new website part of this quote, and do we need one?
- Do you guarantee rankings? (The honest answer is no.)
- Will the monthly report show consult requests, not just rankings and traffic?
- What is the price, the minimum term and the notice period?
- Do we own the website, pages, content and Google Business Profile if we leave?

</Checklist>
