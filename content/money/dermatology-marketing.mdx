The medical side tends to fill through referrals and insurance networks.

The cosmetic side has to be won online, against every med spa, chain, and injector within driving distance.

This page is about that cosmetic side: how dermatology practices use digital marketing to attract cash-pay patients and turn more of those visits into booked consults.

## <span className="he">⚠️</span> Marketing challenges unique to dermatology

Dermatologists have advantages a med spa would pay for: medical training, a physician's name on the door, and a patient base that already trusts them.

They also have problems a med spa doesn't.

## <span className="he">🧭</span> Dermatology marketing ideas that attract new patients

Most dermatology marketing strategies you'll find online are lists of twenty ideas.

These are the ones that move cosmetic bookings, in the order we'd do them.

<Steps>

<Step title="Give cosmetic treatments their own pages">

One page per treatment, with what it does, who performs it, downtime, price guidance, and one clear way to book.

</Step>

<Step title="Make booking easy on a phone">

A short form or an online scheduling link near the top of every treatment page, not a contact page at the bottom of the site.

</Step>

<Step title="Win the local map pack">

A complete Google Business Profile, consistent directory listings, and treatment pages written for your city.

</Step>

<Step title="Answer patient questions with content">

Pages that answer what patients ask before they book, which builds the topical authority search engines reward.

</Step>

<Step title="Pay for new demand with Meta ads">

Once the pages convert, static image ads on Facebook and Instagram send local patients to a landing page built for one offer.

</Step>

</Steps>

The order of these strategies is deliberate.

Every channel does one of two jobs: attract the patient, or convert the visit into a booked consult.

Paying to attract more patients before the page converts sends good money to a leaky site.

## <span className="he">🖥️</span> Your dermatology website: landing pages and conversion

Your website is where every other marketing channel ends up, so it's the first thing to fix.

We don't build or redesign full websites.

We build conversion-focused landing pages and fix what's already on your site, one tested change at a time.

That work is conversion rate optimization (CRO), and here is what a dermatology website needs for the cosmetic side to convert.

<Checklist>

- **A cosmetic menu one click from the homepage,** separate from medical services.
- **One page per treatment** covering results, downtime, price guidance, and who performs it.
- **The dermatologist's credentials on the page,** because medical training is your edge over a med spa.
- **Before-and-after photos with written patient consent** ([photo guide](/blog/medspa-before-after-photos/)).
- **A short form or online booking link** on every treatment page, easy to use on a phone.
- **Fast pages on mobile,** where patients compare practices.
- **Tracking set up with HIPAA in mind** on every form and page.

</Checklist>

Beyond the basics, we add new conversion components: [multi-step forms](/blog/medspa-multi-step-forms/), [exit-intent offers](/blog/medspa-exit-intent-popups/), and landing pages built for a single treatment.

Easy online scheduling is part of the same job, because every extra step between "I want this" and a booked slot loses patients.

When bookings happen inside your scheduling software, we connect it for conversion tracking only if the tracking needs it.

The full method is on the [med spa CRO](/medspa-cro/) page, and it applies to a cosmetic dermatology practice as written.

## <span className="he">💸</span> Paid advertising: why we run Meta ads, not Google Ads

Paid advertising is the fastest way to attract new cosmetic patients, and the fastest way to waste a budget.

Our view, from experience: Google Ads clicks for cosmetic treatments are very expensive, and Meta ads get aesthetic practices better results when they land on a page built to convert.

That's why we run managed Meta ads on Facebook and Instagram, and we don't manage Google Ads or other PPC.

Every month we ship 8–12 fresh static image ads and send them to landing pages we build and A/B test.

The ads run in your own Meta ad account, and your ad spend is paid directly to Meta, never marked up and never a percentage fee.

Brand names like Botox are restricted in ads, so the ads lead with the result and the offer, and the landing page explains the treatment ([healthcare ad policy guide](/blog/medspa-healthcare-ad-policy/)).

How the campaigns are built is on the [med spa advertising](/medspa-advertising/) page.

## <span className="he">📱</span> Social media for dermatologists

Most dermatologist marketing advice says to post more on social media.

We see it differently.

In our experience, organic social posting is slow to produce results and sometimes never does.

We tell practices to put that time into paid ads on Facebook and Instagram, which reach local adults on purpose instead of hoping the algorithm shows your post.

Keep your profiles current, because patients check them before booking, but treat them as a trust signal rather than a booking channel.

We don't manage organic social media or produce video.

## <span className="he">⭐</span> Online reviews and reputation management

Reviews are one of the strongest local ranking signals you control, and patients read them before they book.

We don't run review campaigns, because reviews come from your patients and your front desk.

The advice is short.

- **Ask every patient** for an honest review, ideally at checkout with a direct link.
- **Reply to every review,** good or bad.
- **Protect privacy:** never confirm a reviewer is a patient or mention a treatment in your reply.
- **Stay inside the FTC rules:** no rewards tied to reviews and no filtering for positive ones ([FTC review rules](/blog/ftc-testimonial-rules-medspa/)).

The full system is in [how to get more reviews](/blog/medspa-get-more-reviews/).

## <span className="he">🛡️</span> HIPAA and website tracking

Tracking pixels on a dermatology website can send data that ties a person to a treatment to ad platforms.

That can be an impermissible disclosure of protected health information.

We track conversions on what we build (forms, modals, and landing pages) and set that tracking up with this risk in mind.

We don't claim any setup is certified HIPAA-compliant, so your own compliance advisor should have the final word.

The [HIPAA and website tracking](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/) guide explains what to check.
