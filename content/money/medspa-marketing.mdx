Most med spa marketing advice is a list of channels: run Google Ads, post Reels, try Groupon, send an email blast.

That's not a strategy, it's a to-do list, and it usually leads to spending more on the one lever you control least.

This guide to marketing for med spa owners is the opposite: the owner's mental model first, then the levers in the order that compounds, with the detailed playbooks linked at each step.

It's written for the person who signs the checks at a medical spa, whether that's one location or several.

## <span className="he">🎯</span> The owner's mental model

Every booked consult comes out of one simple equation:

<Equation title="The owner's math" result="Booked consults">
<Term label="Traffic" />
<Term label="Conversion rate" />
<Term label="Show rate" />
</Equation>

Three inputs, three very different costs to improve.

**Traffic** is the most expensive and least controllable.

You rent it with ads, or earn it slowly with SEO and referrals.

**Conversion rate** is nearly free to improve.

It's just your website doing its job better, and every point of lift multiplies against all your traffic at once.

**Show rate** is operations: reminders, deposits, [speed-to-lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-medspa/).

Cheap to fix, quietly expensive to ignore.

Here's why the order matters.

<Bars title="Booked consults per month" caption="Illustrative example: say you get 1,000 visitors a month and convert 3% into booked consults.">
<Bar label="Starting point: 1,000 visitors at 3%" value="50" display="30" />
<Bar label="Double ad budget: 2,000 visitors at 3%" value="100" display="60" />

</Bars>

Doubling the ad budget costs real money every month forever, while lifting conversion costs nothing in ad spend and keeps paying every month after.

Same result, radically different economics.

That's the entire thesis.

## <span className="he">📈</span> Lever one: convert the traffic you already have

Your site is where the money leaks, and the leaks are predictable.

In order of how often they cost the most:

- **The booking flow.** Long single-step forms bleed the highest-intent visitors. [Multi-step forms](/blog/medspa-multi-step-forms/) usually hold them better, because momentum beats commitment shock. This is often the highest-impact test on the site.
- **The call to action.** One clear next step ("Book a consult" or "Claim your first visit"), visible without scrolling on a phone, repeated after every section that answers a question. Pages with five competing buttons convert like pages with none.
- **Intro-offer placement.** The offer that converts a first-time patient is often buried three scrolls down, or framed as a discount instead of a reason to come back. See [intro offers that work](/blog/medspa-intro-offers/).
- **Treatment pages.** Most read like a brochure. They should answer the two questions every prospective patient has: "is this right for me?" and "what does it cost?"
- **Mobile speed and UX.** Most med spa traffic is on a phone, and [a slow mobile booking experience](/blog/medspa-website-speed-mobile/) quietly kills conversions before the form even loads.
- **Visual proof.** [Before-and-after photos](/blog/medspa-before-after-photos/) are the strongest proof an aesthetic practice has, as long as every one has written patient consent.
- **Social proof.** Reviews, patient stories, and provider credentials, placed where the decision actually happens, and within the [FTC's rules on testimonials](/blog/ftc-testimonial-rules-medspa/).

If you're planning a new site or a redesign, our guide to [med spa website design](/blog/medspa-website-design/) covers what to brief your designer on.

The way to fix these without guessing is [conversion rate optimization](/medspa-cro/): one controlled test a month on the highest-impact spot, run long enough to reach statistical significance.

That's also where new conversion components come in, like a multi-step form or an [exit-intent offer](/blog/medspa-exit-intent-popups/), each one tested against what you have now.

<Callout type="key">

One *valid* test a month beats ten sloppy ones. "We tried it for a week and it looked better" isn't a test, it's a guess with a screenshot.

Twelve valid tests a year compound; a dozen half-finished experiments cancel each other out.

Start by knowing your baseline: [what a good med spa conversion rate actually is](/blog/medspa-conversion-rate-benchmarks/).

</Callout>

## <span className="he">🔍</span> Lever two: rank for the treatments your city searches

Once your site converts, more traffic is worth having, and the most durable traffic is local search.

When someone nearby searches "lip filler" or "laser hair removal" plus your city, you want to be the answer.

[Med spa SEO](/medspa-seo/) has two layers:

- **The local map pack.** A fully-completed [Google Business Profile](/blog/medspa-google-business-profile/), steady and recent [reviews](/blog/medspa-get-more-reviews/), and consistent [citations](/blog/medspa-citations-directories/). Google ranks the pack on relevance, distance, and prominence, and reviews are the strongest prominence lever you control.
- **Treatment-by-city page authority.** Pages that genuinely cover the treatments you offer, in the market you serve, so you rank in the organic results below the map too.

The full walkthrough is [how to rank a med spa on Google Maps](/blog/how-to-rank-medspa-google-maps/).

Reviews deserve their own habit at the front desk: ask every happy patient, reply to every review, and never confirm in a reply that the reviewer is a patient.

Unlike ads, local SEO compounds: the work you do this quarter keeps paying next year.

A side effect of building that kind of topical authority is that AI answers, like ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews, start to cite you too.

## <span className="he">🧲</span> Paid ads: why Meta beats Google Ads for most med spas

Local SEO is the durable core, but it's slow, so most practices want paid traffic alongside it.

The question is where to spend.

<Compare>

<Side title="Google Ads" tone="neutral">

- Reaches people already searching for a treatment
- Clicks for aesthetic treatments are very expensive
- Every click you pay for lands on a page that has to convert
- Healthcare ad policies limit what you can say

</Side>

<Side title="Meta ads (Facebook and Instagram)" tone="yes">

- Reaches nearby people before they start searching
- Visual treatments like injectables and laser suit image ads
- Fresh creative every month keeps ads from going stale
- Works best sent to a landing page built for the offer

</Side>

</Compare>

Our view, from experience: Google Ads clicks are very expensive for med spas, and Meta ads tend to get better results.

That's why Meta is the paid channel we run, and Google Ads is not something we manage.

On Meta, the part most practices skip is the page the ad lands on.

[Managed Meta ads](/medspa-advertising/) here means fresh static image ads every month, sent to a landing page we build and A/B test instead of your homepage.

The DIY version is in the [Facebook and Instagram ads guide](/blog/medspa-facebook-instagram-ads/), and [TikTok ads](/blog/medspa-tiktok-ads/) are another paid option some practices test for younger patients.

## <span className="he">💉</span> Offers, promotions, and the treatment calendar

The best clients for most med spas are the ones who come back: neurotoxin patients on a regular schedule, filler patients, and laser series patients.

Build your marketing around the treatments that create repeat visits, not just the ones that are easy to discount.

The treatment playbooks go deeper on each: [Botox marketing](/blog/botox-marketing/), [dermal filler marketing](/blog/dermal-filler-marketing/), and [laser hair removal marketing](/blog/laser-hair-removal-marketing/), plus the rest in [marketing by treatment](/blog/treatment-marketing-medspa/).

Promotions work best when they're planned, not reactive.

Laid out as a calendar, they follow the treatments' own seasons: for example, laser hair removal series often start in fall or winter so patients finish before summer.

Check your own booking history for when demand rises, then time promotions and SEO work a few months ahead of it.

Keep the offer a reason to start, not a permanent discount, and [price](/blog/medspa-pricing-strategy/) so a first visit can become a regular one.

## <span className="he">🔁</span> Retention: the lever owners leave on the table

The cheapest booking is the one you already earned.

A patient who comes back regularly is worth many times a one-time visit, and predictable revenue changes what you can afford to spend on acquisition.

That's the idea behind [patient lifetime value](/blog/medspa-patient-lifetime-value/).

Retention runs on three things:

- [Membership programs](/blog/medspa-membership-programs/) that convert one-time visits into recurring revenue; launch to existing patients first as a founding-member offer
- [Email](/blog/medspa-email-marketing/) and [SMS](/blog/medspa-sms-marketing/) sequences that rebook lapsed patients
- A front-desk [rebooking habit](/blog/medspa-client-retention/) so the next appointment is booked before the current one ends

The same goes for new leads: [how fast your team calls back](/blog/speed-to-lead-medspa/) decides how many of them ever become a booked consult.

The broader picture, acquisition plus retention, is laid out in [how to get more clients for your med spa](/blog/how-to-get-more-medspa-clients/).

## <span className="he">📊</span> Tracking: measure bookings, not likes

Marketing you can't measure turns into opinion.

The metric that matters is booked consults, and then cost per booked consult against what a patient is worth over time.

Clicks, followers, and impressions only matter if they move that number.

The core set is in [med spa KPIs](/blog/medspa-kpis/), and connecting leads back to their source is in [attribution and call tracking](/blog/medspa-attribution-call-tracking/).

On our side, conversions are tracked on everything we build: forms, modals, and landing pages.

When it's needed, we connect your booking software for conversion tracking only, so a test is judged on bookings rather than clicks.

## <span className="he">⚖️</span> Compliance sits under everything

Med spa marketing touches advertising law, privacy law, and platform rules, and most violations come down to a few avoidable habits.

<Checklist>

- Never guarantee results in ads, on the site, or in a caption
- Get written consent before using any patient photo or story
- Follow the FTC's rules for testimonials, reviews, and influencer disclosure
- Keep claims about injectables and devices within what they're approved for
- Keep patient information out of ad pixels and analytics tools
- Get consent before texting patients, and follow CAN-SPAM for email

</Checklist>

Tracking pixels are the quiet risk: they can send patient information to ad platforms without anyone noticing, which is covered in [HIPAA and website tracking](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/).

The full picture is in [med spa marketing laws and regulations](/blog/medspa-marketing-laws/).

This is general guidance, not legal advice, so run anything uncertain past a healthcare attorney.
