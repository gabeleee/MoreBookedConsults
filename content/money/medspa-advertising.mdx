## <span className="he">🪣</span> Why the ads don't go to your website

Every med spa owner who's been burned by advertising has the same story: the ads ran, the clicks came, the bookings didn't.

The usual diagnosis, "the ads were bad," is usually wrong.

The ads sent people to a site that leaked them.

At a typical booking rate, most of your ad spend funds the 97 percent who bounce.

<Callout type="key">

Ads are only as good as the page they land on.

That is why the traffic here lands on dedicated landing pages built from the same testing playbook that grew sitewide conversion 3% → 11% at [LaserAway](https://www.laseraway.com), across 2,600+ tested variations. The page does the converting. The ads just fill it.

</Callout>

Your website doesn't need to change.

It isn't where the paid traffic goes.

## <span className="he">🎯</span> How managed Meta ads work

<Steps>

<Step title="Tracking first">

Before a dollar is spent, I set up the Meta pixel and Conversions API so every lead and booking is measured correctly. Without clean tracking, Meta optimizes toward the wrong people and every report is guesswork.

</Step>

<Step title="One offer, one landing page">

We pick the treatment to lead with (Botox and laser hair removal are the usual starting points) and I build a landing page for that single offer. One clear promise, a short path, no navigation bar full of exits.

</Step>

<Step title="Fresh static ads every month">

8–12 new static image ads a month, written and designed to test different angles, offers, and hooks. Creative fatigue is the quiet killer of Meta campaigns, so the ads never sit long enough to go stale.

</Step>

<Step title="One landing-page test a month">

Every month the landing page gets an A/B test: headline, offer framing, form length, proof. The ads find the people, and the tests make the page better at turning them into consults.

</Step>

<Step title="A report you can read">

A plain-English monthly report: what ran, what won, what lost, what changes next month. No 40-slide deck, no vanity metrics.

</Step>

</Steps>

## <span className="he">🧮</span> How to think about the budget

The minimum ad spend is $3,000 a month because below that, Meta rarely gets enough conversions to optimize, and the tests take too long to read.

Run the math the way you already price your chair time.

If a Botox patient is worth $600 a visit and comes back three times a year, you know roughly what a new patient is worth to you.

## <span className="he">⚡</span> The part only your team can do

Paid leads go cold fast.

A lead called in 5 minutes behaves very differently from a lead called in 5 hours, and [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-medspa/) is the single biggest factor in whether ad leads book.

The landing page can capture the interest.

Your front desk has to turn it into a consult.

If your team can't reliably call within business hours, tell me up front, because it changes how the offer and form should be built.
