## <span className="he">🪣</span> Why the ads don't go to your website

Every med spa owner who's been burned by advertising has the same story: the ads ran, the clicks came, the bookings didn't.

The usual diagnosis, "the ads were bad," is usually wrong.

The ads sent people to a site that leaked them.

A practice website is built to inform patients who already know you, with a menu of treatments, an about page, and a dozen exits.

A stranger who just saw an offer on Instagram needs one thing: a fast, clear way to claim it.

## <span className="he">🏷️</span> The offer drives the ads

The strongest creative in the world can't rescue a weak offer.

Before any ads are made, we pick one treatment and one reason to book now.

Botox, dermal fillers, and laser hair removal are the usual leads, because patients already know what they are and many come back.

Seasonal promotions and treatment bundles work well too, as long as the offer is simple enough to understand in one glance.

The [intro offers guide](/blog/medspa-intro-offers/) covers how to structure one without training clients to wait for discounts.

Creative for a medical spa has to balance two things at once: medical credibility and a luxury brand feel.

Too clinical and the ad feels cold.

Too glossy and it reads like a salon, not a practice with a medical director.

The best ads name who does the treatment, keep the promise realistic, and look like your practice.

## <span className="he">🛡️</span> What med spa ads can and can't say

Aesthetic ads sit inside health advertising rules, and getting them wrong can get an ad rejected or an account restricted.

Here is the short version, in general terms, with the details linked.

<Checklist>

- **Prescription-only treatments:** Botox and other neuromodulators are prescription drugs, and brand names are restricted on Meta and Google, so the ad sells the outcome and the landing page explains the treatment.
- **Before-and-after photos:** Meta's rules on them have changed more than once, and every photo needs the patient's specific written consent ([before-and-after photo guide](/blog/medspa-before-after-photos/)).
- **Claims:** no copy that attacks the viewer's appearance, no guaranteed results, no timeframe promises without qualifiers.
- **Targeting:** cosmetic procedure ads target adults, and health-based audience targeting is limited.
- **Tracking:** a pixel on the wrong page can send patient information to an ad platform ([HIPAA and website tracking](/blog/hipaa-website-tracking/)).

</Checklist>

The full rules, with links to Meta's and Google's own policy pages, are in the [healthcare ad policy guide](/blog/medspa-healthcare-ad-policy/).

Rules change, so every campaign is checked against current policy before launch.

## <span className="he">🧮</span> How to think about the budget

The minimum ad spend is $3,000 a month because below that, Meta rarely gets enough conversions to optimize, and the tests take too long to read.

Run the math the way you already price your chair time.

If a Botox patient is worth $600 a visit and comes back three times a year, you know roughly what a new patient is worth to you.

That number is what makes ROAS (return on ad spend) mean something for a med spa.

ROAS compares the revenue your ads produce with what you spent on them.

Measured on first visits alone, it undercounts, because a patient worth $1,800 a year shows up as $600 on the day they book.

Measure it on what a patient is worth over time, and the question becomes simple: are the ads producing patients worth more than they cost?

That, not a promised cost per lead, is what tells you whether the channel is working.

I won't promise a lead count or a cost per lead, because those depend on your market, your offer, and how fast your team calls people back.

What the report will show you every month is exactly what the budget bought.

## <span className="he">⚡</span> The part only your team can do

Paid leads go cold fast.

A lead called in 5 minutes behaves very differently from a lead called in 5 hours, and in my experience [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-medspa/) decides more ad leads than anything else.

The landing page can capture the interest.

Your front desk has to turn it into a consult.

That means someone owns new leads every day, calls them back fast, and follows up more than once before giving up.

It also means confirming the appointment and reminding the patient before it, so a booked consult actually shows up.

If your team can't reliably call within business hours, tell me up front, because it changes how the offer and form should be built.
