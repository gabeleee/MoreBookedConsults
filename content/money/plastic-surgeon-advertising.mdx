## <span className="he">🪣</span> Why the ads don't go to your website

Plastic surgery has some of the most expensive clicks in all of advertising, because a single surgery is worth so much and incumbents bid hard.

Which makes the leak worse here than anywhere: when those premium clicks land on a site that loses them to weak galleries, buried financing, or slow follow-up, you pay twice.

Once for the click, once for the surgery you didn't book.

<Callout type="key">

Ads are only as good as the page they land on.

So every campaign here sends traffic to a dedicated consult landing page built from the same testing playbook Gabe used to grow sitewide conversion 3% → 11% at [LaserAway](https://www.laseraway.com), across 2,600+ tested variations. The page is where the money is won or lost. The ads just fill it.

</Callout>

Your website doesn't need to change.

It isn't where the ads point.

## <span className="he">🎯</span> How managed ads work

**1. Setup: tracking, a landing page, and the first ads.** I install the Meta pixel and the Conversions API so every consult request is measured properly, build your first consult landing page, and produce the first batch of static ads.

**2. Launch on Facebook and Instagram.** Campaigns run in your own ad account, on your card, so you own the account, the data, and the history.

**3. Fresh ads every month.** 8 to 12 new static image ads a month, rotating through outcomes, surgeon credibility, financing, and consult offers, so creative fatigue never quietly drains the budget.

**4. One landing-page test every month.** Headline, gallery placement, financing, form length: one controlled A/B test at a time, so the page keeps getting better at turning a click into a consult request.

**5. A report you can read.** Every month you get a plain-English summary of spend, consult requests, what won, what lost, and what I'm changing next.
