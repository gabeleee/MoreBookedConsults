Almost every med spa has run Facebook ads, and almost every one has a story about how they didn't work.

The clicks came, the money went out, the bookings didn't follow.

The reflex is to blame the ads, the targeting, or Facebook itself.

Usually none of those is the real culprit.

The ads did their job and sent interested people somewhere.

The problem is where they sent them.

## <span className="he">🪣</span> The destination is where med spa Facebook ads die

A Facebook ad has exactly one job: get an interested stranger to click.

What happens after the click decides whether you get a lead or just a bill.

Most practices send that click to their website, which is the single most expensive habit in med spa advertising.

Your website was built to inform a patient who already knows you and is deciding between treatments.

A cold stranger who just saw a Botox or laser hair removal ad is a different visitor with a different need: one clear offer, a short path, and a reason to act now.

Drop them on a homepage and you hand them a navigation bar and a dozen exits.

<Callout type="key">

Improving the ad while leaving the destination broken is polishing the wrong half of the machine.

The highest-return change in most med spa Facebook advertising is not a better ad. It is a better place for the click to land.

</Callout>

## <span className="he">🖼️</span> Why static ads, and why so many

Creative fatigue is the quiet killer of med spa Facebook campaigns.

The same audience sees the same ad for weeks, the cost of each result creeps up, and the owner decides "Facebook stopped working."

The fix is fresh creative on a steady schedule.

The campaigns here ship 8–12 new static image ads every month, each one testing a different angle, offer, or hook.

Static ads are quick to produce and quick to read, so the winners get more budget and the losers get cut before they waste much.

Volume of good tests beats production value almost every time in this category.

## <span className="he">📋</span> The Botox and injectables ad-policy catch

Injectables are where generic ad operators get practices in trouble.

Name the drug the wrong way and the ad policies flag or reject it.

The craft is advertising the outcome and the offer while staying inside the rules, then letting the landing page do the specific qualifying once the person is engaged.

This is exactly the kind of nuance a CRO operator handles and a template shop reusing one page across industries does not.

It is also why the treatment pages, like [Botox lead generation](/botox-lead-generation/), exist: the offer structure is treatment-specific, not one-size-fits-all.
